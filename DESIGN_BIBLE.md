# Helix Heresy Design Bible

This document is the living design bible for Helix Heresy. It collects story background, design goals, current prototype notes, future systems, and open questions. It is allowed to be longer and more speculative than the main README.

## Story

Cosmology

The gods are real and can physically manifest, grant blessings, perform miracles, and wage war against one another. Most people believe the gods are eternal beings, but this is false. Every god was originally a mortal creature that accumulated enough worship, belief, and soul influence to ascend beyond mortality. The gods actively suppress this knowledge because widespread awareness of ascension would create countless cults, prophets, and would-be gods.

Divine Power

Gods are not self-sustaining. They constantly expend power simply by existing, and miracles, manifestations, divine servants, celestial realms, and divine wars all consume enormous amounts of energy. Worship is the primary fuel that sustains them. Without worshippers, a god gradually weakens and can eventually cease to exist. Because of this, gods compete relentlessly for worshippers, souls, territory, and influence.

Souls and Heroes

Souls are the foundation of the divine ecosystem. Mortals live, worship, die, and pass through systems controlled by the gods. Exceptional individuals become heroes whose accomplishments attract admiration, loyalty, and worship. That worship further empowers them, allowing them to perform feats beyond normal mortals. After death, gods compete to recruit heroic souls into their service, making them valuable divine assets.

Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering is outlawed throughout most of the world. Publicly it is condemned as unnatural and dangerous. In reality, the gods oppose it because artificial life threatens the divine economy. Soulless creatures provide no worship, contribute nothing to any god, and exist outside the normal cycle of life and death. The threat becomes even greater when artificial creatures kill mortals, as every dead worshipper represents lost faith, lost descendants, and lost future power for the gods.

Animancy

Animancy is the study and manipulation of souls and is one of the most forbidden fields of knowledge in existence. Most animancers seek to understand souls, while the most dangerous attempt to create, alter, transfer, manufacture, or control them. The gods fear animancy because it interferes directly with the source of divine power. A sufficiently advanced animancer could create artificial worshippers, manufacture loyal souls, establish entirely new religions, and potentially elevate a mortal being into godhood.

The Scientist

The protagonist is not trying to become a god and does not understand how the gods actually function. They are simply an obsessive and brilliant scientist who wants to create increasingly sophisticated organisms. During their research they accidentally invent a machine capable of combining genetic engineering and animancy, allowing artificial creatures to be created with genuine souls. To the scientist this is merely a scientific breakthrough. To the gods it is an existential threat.

The Clone Contingency

The player character is a young-adult clone of the original scientist. By the end of the original scientist's life, their forbidden knowledge and accomplishments had made capture and death inevitable. They created a phylactery, prepared a younger clone in a synthesis chamber, and arranged for their soul to transfer into that body when the original died. The younger body was chosen deliberately: authorities searching for the older, publicly recognizable scientist would not immediately identify the clone when they encountered them outside the laboratory. The resemblance is still dangerous. Someone who knew the scientist when young, genetic testing, soul identification, old biometric records, or sufficiently advanced magic could expose the connection.

The original scientist expected the transfer to preserve complete personal continuity. They did not know that the phylactery would preserve the soul's animantic identity while losing the memories and practiced skills encoded by the original brain. The clone therefore awakens with the same soul but without the experience that made the original accomplished. Residual magical sensitivity and damaged procedural instincts support the scientist's few level-one starting skills, but expertise must be rebuilt through actual practice. The transfer exhausts the prepared phylactery and does not provide an automatic cycle of replacement bodies.

The Starting Laboratory

The contingency is hidden in one of the original scientist's oldest abandoned laboratories. The site was useful because it was forgotten, disconnected from the scientist's later operations, and unlikely to appear in an investigation of their mature work. The original secretly returned only to install the phylactery, young clone, and transfer apparatus; they did not restore the rest of the laboratory or prepare it for an inexperienced successor.

Everything else in the starting laboratory comes from the original scientist's formative period: primitive equipment, basic slime experiments, early samples, abandoned materials, incomplete trials, and notes written while they were still learning. Those records are historical working notes written for the original scientist and any contemporary collaborators, not tutorial messages for an amnesiac clone. They must not contain convenient warnings such as "if you are reading this, the transfer failed." The original believed they would awaken remembering how everything worked.

This is why an accomplished scientist begins gameplay with basic slimes, obsolete tools, and almost no usable skill. The clone must reconstruct the original's expertise by retracing the path through their earliest work. The conspicuously advanced soul-transfer apparatus surrounded by an otherwise antiquated laboratory should reinforce both the original scientist's later power and the magnitude of their mistaken assumption about memory.

Long-Term Story

The laboratory begins as an illegal biotechnology operation focused on creating increasingly sophisticated lifeforms. Over time, creature intelligence increases and some creations become self-aware, form societies, develop cultures, and create belief systems. Many eventually conclude that the being who designed them, created them, and shapes their world must be divine. The scientist never intends to create a religion, but worship from their creations gradually elevates their soul. What begins as a hidden laboratory eventually becomes a rival source of divinity, culminating in a conflict between a self-made god of biotechnology and the existing divine order.

## Slime Biology

All slimes are magical by nature. They are not artificial organisms; outside the laboratory, slimes are one of the most numerous natural organism families in the world. The laboratory does not invent slimes, but synthesizes, studies, modifies, accelerates, and redirects existing slime biology. Elemental affinity is not what makes a slime magical. It is a specialized magical expression within an already magical organism, so a slime with no elemental affinity is still magical.

Slimes are the starter creature family because they are simple, durable, adaptable, and biologically permissive. “Slime” is a broad category, not a single body type. Some are gelatinous, but others may be watery, rubbery, crystalline, tar-like, waxen, clay-like, fibrous, foamy, or stranger forms. Their shape, consistency, appendages, element, sustenance, and behavior determine how they function.

Slimes are asexual scavenger organisms that can consume almost anything, but not necessarily well. Each slime has a primary Sustenance adaptation shaped by natural evolution and reproduction, but mismatched food or resources may digest slowly, injure the slime, leave residue behind, create contamination, or produce low-quality outputs. Specialized slimes are valuable because they perform the same biological work faster, cleaner, and with less risk.

Loose feeding now follows that same Sustenance logic in the prototype. A loose slime can make a good, partial, poor, or harmful match against local residue, loose waste, or accessible remains. Good matches feed and regrow the slime more cleanly, while poor or harmful matches are slower, raise Stress or Body Integrity risk, and can leave contaminated or hazardous feeding residue behind.

A slime’s body is usually distributed and partially replaceable. This makes many slimes resistant to ordinary physical damage, since cutting, crushing, puncturing, melting, or removing part of the body may not kill the creature. The exact form of resilience depends on body consistency. A watery slime, brittle crystalline slime, rubbery slime, and tar-like slime should not all react to damage the same way. Ordinary harm should damage body integrity, reduce efficiency, increase stress, or make jobs harder, but it should not automatically shorten natural lifespan.

Each slime has a core that functions as its vital biological center and animantic anchor. The body can often be damaged or partially destroyed while the slime survives, but destruction of the core kills the creature. The core is also a natural place to connect future systems such as soul imprinting, animancy, necropsy value, core corruption, cloning, mutation instability, and catastrophic job failure.

Slimes reproduce primarily by splitting rather than sexual breeding. A mature slime that consumes enough material can become nutritionally saturated, and sustained fullness creates reproductive pressure. This does not mean a slime instantly splits the moment it eats enough. Instead, the slime must reach full current mass and remain full, fed, stable, and low-stress long enough for Division Pressure to accumulate. Depending on the slime’s traits, reproduction may look like splitting, budding, fracturing, shedding, leaking off daughter masses, or separating into offspring.

A mature slime can only split when it has reached its natural maximum size. The genetic Size trait represents the slime’s target mature size, while Current Mass can temporarily fall below that target after injury, starvation, or reproduction. Nutrition and feeding allow a slime to rebuild toward its natural maximum, but being well-fed is not enough to split if the slime has not fully regrown.

Slime splitting obeys strict mass conservation. A slime does not magically create new biomatter when it divides; it divides its existing body mass between the parent and offspring. Brood Size determines how many offspring a natural split tries to produce, so high-brood slimes create more bodies but each body begins smaller and weaker. After a normal split, the parent is temporarily reduced in size and the offspring begin below full mature size. Both must feed and regrow before they can reach their natural maximum. Because splitting requires full size and sustained fullness, repeated splitting without enough food is impossible rather than merely producing smaller and smaller slimes.

Natural slime reproduction is unstable but deterministic from the simulation seed and circumstances. Offspring inherit the parent's 26-base haploid genome with a meaningful, condition-dependent chance of zero or more mutations; mutation is possible rather than mandatory. Brood Size controls the attempted offspring count. Growth speed, hidden stability, body integrity, Stress, contamination, Ambient Mana, and laboratory conditions influence timing or variability. A natural brood is resolved as a whole: if its container lacks volume or a loose parent lacks safe floor space, Division Pressure remains full and creates an understandable pressure problem instead of silently deleting offspring.

The implemented system has three methods. Natural Division is autonomous, single-parent, and the least predictable. Induced Division is a controlled single-parent procedure with the highest baseline fidelity. Forced Recombination is a controlled two-parent procedure that inherits contiguous base segments from both parents and carries greater instability. Controlled procedures offer Fidelity, Balanced, and Novelty priorities and one-to-three offspring. They consume physical Genetic Material and scientist Stamina, route the scientist past every parent and then to the Synthesis Tube, reserve one permanent destination per child, and preserve parent-plus-child mass exactly. Larger controlled broods begin smaller and impose greater strain.

Mutation is not pure chaos and does not reveal hidden gene-to-trait mappings. The Lineage workspace may show raw inherited base ranges, exact de novo mutation positions, method, priority, generation, brood, parents, children, starting condition, and broad procedure context. Every completed brood creates an immutable reproduction event and reusable research evidence; descendants remain ordinary Experiment Notebook subjects. Hidden stability and trait meaning continue to require observation and experimentation.

The practical rule for slimes is that they are generalist scavengers by default and specialists by design. Any slime can attempt crude jobs like corpse consumption, waste disposal, hazardous cleanup, or basic production, but traits should determine whether the job is safe, fast, efficient, or useful. Feeding slimes is therefore not just maintenance; it can also create reproductive pressure, storage problems, mutation opportunities, and new resource-management decisions. Sustenance describes the primary pathway a slime uses to turn matter, waste, decay, or environmental resources into Nutrition and Current Mass; environmental sustenance is deliberately slow until room and container resources exist. Slime biology should support the larger game loop: create strange organisms, study their traits, feed and use them, manage the consequences of their reproduction and waste, and breed or engineer better versions.

Habitat fit is derived from existing biology rather than being its own gene. Element, body consistency, Sustenance, behavior, and stability can make a slime prefer or tolerate certain levels of Temperature, Light, Ambient Mana, Humidity, Contamination, or Electrical Charge. The readout stays broad, such as Ideal, Comfortable, Tolerable, Poor Fit, or Hostile. Known traits explain known supports and concerns; hidden traits remain hidden, but they can still affect the real organism.

Threat response is derived from condition and biology rather than being a separate gene. Stress, Body Integrity, Nutrition, Current Mass, recent injury, containment risk, habitat mismatch, behavior, and stability can make a slime calm, wary, agitated, pained, panicked, or desperate. The UI shows a broad likely action such as watch, hide, freeze, flee, lash out, seek food desperately, recover, or endure. Hidden behavior and stability can still shape the real response, but the readout should list them as unknown factors until discovered instead of revealing the trait.

Group behavior treats slime social behavior as proximity biology rather than human-like society. Slimes track current nearby bodies, physical contact, kin, non-kin, hunger, stress, broad behavior, and stability to produce a simple Group Behavior readout. Current stances include isolated, brood cohesion, protective, tolerant, crowded, competing, avoiding, territorial, and hostile. Current action tendencies include cohere, separate, align, compete, protect, avoid, patrol, expel, scatter, and rally. These are local temporary states, not separate genetic traits, permanent factions, or a shared group mind.

Loose-slime neighborhoods are physically bounded to six tiles and retain no more than 16 detailed neighbors. Additional local bodies contribute aggregate density without creating per-slime pair work or identity knowledge. Compatible bodies form deterministic temporary connected cohorts that dissolve when proximity changes. Social and brood-oriented slimes may cohere or align; dense groups separate; hungry non-kin compete; solitary slimes avoid; and territorial slimes remember a personal anchor that slowly strengthens during settled occupation and decays over time. Damage emits a physical slime-distress chemical trace into the injured slime's tile or container. The trace diffuses, leaks through physical boundaries, and decays through the environment model; nearby slimes may protect kin, rally, or scatter only when the trace can physically reach them. Reactions do not recursively emit distress.

Natural Division, Induced Division, and Forced Recombination preserve lineage through parent IDs, child links, brood IDs, generation numbers, and immutable reproduction-event IDs. Parent/offspring and brood siblings recognize one another as protected kin. They may still crowd each other, compete with non-kin nearby, or suffer passive elemental clash damage if their bodies are physically incompatible, but they do not intentionally attack or feed-attack parents, offspring, or brood siblings. Non-kin crowding can create light Stress over time and can push hostile, territorial, avoidant, or competitive behavior depending on current biology and condition.

Elemental damage tags are the shared vocabulary for physical and magical hazards. The prototype currently derives them only from Element, not byproduct: none, stone, metal, and wood are Physical; acid is Corrosive; poison is Toxic; flame is Heat; frost is Cold; storm is Electrical; water is Moisture; wind is Pressure; light is Radiant; shadow is Shadow; dream and ether are Arcane; gravity is Force; null is Arcane and Force. Once Element is discovered, the slime panel shows broad tags and exposure descriptions. Containers and handling tools have broad resistance summaries that current tool durability and future combat, room damage, and containment wear can reuse without exposing exact formulas.

Combat is map-aware and real-time rather than a separate tactical arena or turn mode. Combat records live in `state.combat.active` and feed the normal incident system. Physical contact currently means released slimes sharing occupied footprint tiles or living slimes sharing the same container; diagonal footprint edges count as melee adjacency. Opposed elemental contact can create passive clash damage over time. Slime combat behavior uses qualitative intents including avoid, defend, threaten, attack, feed-attack, panic, flee, and freeze. Hunger, injury, stress, recent pain, pain memory, hunting behavior, stability, target type, contact, and protected kinship shape the intent. Protected kin suppress intentional attacks, although incompatible elements can still clash passively. If the scientist perceives active combat, the game switches to 1x and pauses so the player can respond.

Combat actions use a shared actor model for targeting, range, line of effect, damage types, skill contests, condition, movement, windup, recovery, and defensive stance. Scientist actions include Strike, Guard, Shove, Retreat, Intercept, Soul Lash, Analyze, and Combat Analyze. Strike spends Stamina and practices Striking rather than Creature Handling. Guard suspends routine work and reduces incoming damage. Shove uses Grappling and Force to displace an adjacent loose target when the destination is physically open. Retreat urgently moves one tile away. Intercept is a Guarding reaction prepared against a named target and triggers once when that creature attacks. Soul Lash remains a short-range Animancy attack that spends Mana and deals Arcane damage to a living souled target with a clear line of effect. Prepared actions retain their last-known target and can fail when it moves or is lost.

Damage can create saved anatomy-aware injuries. Scientists use head, torso, arms, and legs; slime injuries use core, body mass, membrane, appendages where appropriate, and elemental structures. Injury families include bruising, bleeding, burns, corrosion, electrical trauma, fractures, arcane or soul trauma, membrane tears, and core trauma at Minor through Critical severity. Location and severity influence movement, evasion, attacks, and work duration. Progressive bleeding, corrosion, and membrane leakage continue until stabilized, and leakage can contaminate the room. Critical head, torso, or core trauma and very low positive vitality can incapacitate an actor before death; the scientist still ends the run at zero Health, while creatures still become corpses at zero Body Integrity.

Known visible wounds can be treated directly, while uncertain internal trauma requires examination before its nature is shown. Examination, stabilization, and treatment are ordinary routed scientist tasks using Medicine, Stamina, physical access to the patient, and reserved consumable supplies. Bandages support pressure dressings and splinting, neutralizing wash supports burns, corrosion, electrical, and arcane contact, and membrane sealant supports slime tears and core trauma. Stabilization stops progression; treatment begins timed recovery; appropriate rest accelerates a scientist's treated recovery. This is first aid and biological sealing only: equipment loadouts, armor, surgery, infection, and advanced medical facilities remain separate future systems.

Every ability definition can specify charge time, channel time, recovery time, movement and damage interruption rules, Mana timing at activation, gradually, or release, legal target kinds, range, line of effect, and whether the target must possess a soul. Most magical abilities should be effectively instant, but some may deliberately charge or build up before release. Prepared actions preserve the exact perceived target location; they do not track a creature through hidden information. If the target moves or is lost before release, the action fails at its last-known position. Direct combat movement and prepared actions can interrupt routine scientist work without deleting it; routine tasks resume with their remaining schedule preserved afterward.

Combat and emergency response have a dedicated Combat map overlay separate from the general Incidents overlay. The Combat overlay shows known active or last-known combat incident tiles and lightly marks known participants when an active observed combat record is still available. Clicking a combat marker selects the combat incident record first, preserving uncertainty and keeping the incident as the response surface. The Selection inspector's Combat Situation section shows status, awareness, last-known tile, summary, known participants, combat type, contact, and response state without revealing hidden fights, exact formulas, unobserved weaknesses, or undiagnosed internal wounds. Responding to a combat incident queues urgent scientist movement toward the last-known incident tile rather than inventing a safest approach. Combat and medical actions remain normal contextual commands with physical disabled reasons; actor inspectors show known active injuries and incapacity without opening a separate combat screen.

## Containment Emergencies

Containment emergencies are saved physical situations rather than temporary popups. Each breached source owns an individual emergency record while every active source in the same room contributes to a compact room-level coordination summary. The lifecycle is Warning, Breach Imminent, Breached, Uncontrolled, Secured, Recovery, and Resolved. A loose creature continues using ordinary drives, movement, group behavior, combat, feeding, and sensory rules; the emergency record observes those facts instead of replacing its AI with a scripted escape sequence.

Player awareness remains knowledge-safe. The scientist can discover an emergency through ordinary exact or directional perception, while an operational wall-mounted Containment Alarm reports emergencies originating in its own room. Imprecise perception reports an unconfirmed emergency without exposing exact subjects, recovery requirements, or stage. Warnings notify without changing time. Serious and critical responses follow the selected policy: Manual leaves time and doors alone, Notify and Pause returns serious events to 1x and pauses critical events at 1x, and Automatic Lockdown also activates emergency access restrictions and queues each door's configured physical lockdown operation. No policy remotely changes an ordinary door.

The existing incident inspector is the response surface. It exposes investigation movement, configured lockdown, physical retreat, bait placement, known-creature recapture, hazardous cleanup, source repair, message history, and task management as context permits. Combat actions remain on known combatants and the scientist. Recapture does not resolve the emergency by itself: linked creatures must be secured, serious creature injuries must enter recovery, the breached source must be repaired and resealed, room contamination reduced, hazardous spills cleaned, and tagged response work cleared. Stage history and the ordinary persistent damage, residue, contamination, injury, welfare, event, and Suspicion state provide the lasting consequence record; deeper institutional investigation remains a later system.

## Creature Welfare

Creature welfare is a renderer-independent longitudinal layer derived from existing physical simulation state. It does not add redundant survival bars or a Social gene. The five player-facing needs are Nourishment, Recovery, Habitat, Social Fit, and Stimulation, each reported only as Met, Strained, Deprived, or Critical. Nutrition, Current Mass, Body Integrity, Stress, recent activity and pain, environmental fit, spatial pressure, real proximity, kinship, crowding, and access to reusable enrichment remain authoritative.

Social preference is derived from behavior, stability, brood context, and current relationships. The broad preferences are Solitary, Tolerant, Social, Brood-Oriented, and Territorial. Hidden biology affects the real result, while the ordinary record says that relevant factors are uncertain until observation or testing supports a more specific explanation. Social Fit never grants room-wide awareness: only actual shared-container contact or physical room proximity counts.

Stimulation comes from movement, exploration, meaningful work, environmental variety, and the physical Slime Enrichment Station. The station is reusable equipment with a map footprint and ordinary construction/material rules; it supports nearby creatures automatically rather than creating a repetitive toy-clicking action. Inactivity accumulates across hours and days, while meaningful activity refreshes the creature's saved activity history.

Brief problems first express through existing Stress and behavior. Sustained deprivation accumulates deterministic exposure history and can develop Malnourished, Exhausted, Habitat-Sick, Compressed, Understimulated, Overstimulated, Socially Distressed, or Chronically Stressed conditions. Conditions progress through Early, Established, and Severe stages and recede only after sustained correction. Severe physical deprivation gradually damages condition in accumulated, visible increments, so perceived established or severe problems create warning incidents before they can become fatal.

Quiescence is the slime form of autonomous rest. It improves Recovery when nourishment and habitat are adequate; the Recovery care plan increases gradual recovery and suspends ordinary creature work and controlled reproduction. Standard Care balances routine housing and work, Research Exemption permits stressful research while preserving its full recorded burden, and Minimal Intervention avoids unnecessary handling without hiding critical neglect. Care plans do not override combat, physical access, biology, or emergency behavior.

Living tests, tissue harvests, and controlled reproduction write bounded physical-burden entries to each creature's care history. The ledger is qualitative in normal UI and is explicitly not a morality meter. Aggressive procedures may remain faster or productive, but their recent burden and persistent conditions reduce reliable byproduct output, ordinary work, and reproduction quality, and become Experiment Notebook confounders when they change outside the intended treatment.

The current implementation is slime-first but the welfare data contract is generic. It deliberately excludes infection, contagious disease, surgery, pregnancy, souls, advanced psychology, and government enforcement. Those systems may later consume the same physical-care facts without redefining welfare as ethics points or exposing hidden traits.

## Natural Byproducts and Collection

A slime’s natural byproduct is part of its biology. It is separate from feeding residue and separate from material harvested from the slime’s body.

Natural byproduct describes what a living specimen naturally leaves behind or emits as part of its ordinary metabolism. Feeding residue describes the mess left because of what the slime ate, such as loose biomatter after corpse consumption. Harvestable material describes what can be extracted from the slime itself, such as tissue, glands, membranes, cores, or other specimen parts. These systems can interact later, but they should not be collapsed into one trait.

Current prototype feeding residue is physical local mess, not clean inventory. Intended feedstock digestion is tidy and produces no residue. Mismatched or risky manual feeding, uncontrolled loose feeding, corpse consumption, Corpse Processing, and Waste Disposal can leave tagged residue or waste batches in the relevant container or on an exact room tile. Current residue categories include loose biomatter, contaminated residue, inert residue, elemental trace residue, slime trace, and hazardous sludge. Loose spills remain stationary, but any airborne material they release enters the tile environment under its exact identity. Liquid spreading, surface wetness, and evaporation remain future fluid-simulation work. Residue is intentionally separate from natural byproducts, Elemental Residue stockpiles, and harvested specimen material.

Current prototype harvesting is an intentional queued procedure. Living specimens can be sampled, partially harvested, or broken down. Sampling and partial harvest recover a small amount of harvested specimen material while reducing Body Integrity, Current Mass, and/or increasing Stress; breaking down a living specimen consumes it completely. Corpses can also be sampled, harvested, or broken down if they have not been ruined by necropsy or another destructive procedure. Corpse freshness affects harvest yield, partial corpse harvest ruins the corpse for further harvest, and corpse breakdown removes the corpse entirely. Harvested materials become physical Storage Room records with broad material labels and tags, and appear under the derived Harvested Specimen Materials view, separate from Collected Byproducts and local feeding residue.

Natural byproducts should be biologically coherent. A slime’s element, affinity, body consistency, and physiology constrain what byproducts make sense. Acid slimes should express acid-appropriate outputs, water slimes should express water-appropriate outputs, mineral or earthen slimes should express mineral-appropriate outputs, and so on. Byproducts should not be selected from an unrestricted global list unless a future mutation, hybridization, or unstable-experiment system explicitly justifies the mismatch.

Byproduct genes still matter internally, but player-facing UI should not explain gene-slot mapping. The scientist observes byproduct results, output intensity, and collection fit, not the underlying allele table. The same gene pair should have stable internal meaning, and multiple gene pairs can produce the same apparent byproduct while differing in output intensity.

Natural byproduct output has broad bands: Trace, Low, Moderate, and High. Moderate output is the baseline metabolic demand. Lower output reduces food demand slightly, while higher output increases food demand slightly. Exact scalar values, exact food modifiers, and hidden individual expression rolls should remain hidden during normal play.

The genome defines the possible byproduct and output band, but an individual specimen can still roll a hidden expression value inside that band when created. This supports a creature-hunting loop where a specimen can have good genes and still be more or less exceptional as an individual.

Current biological condition shapes how much of that potential is actually expressed. Nutrition, Current Mass, Body Integrity, Stress, and maturity affect natural byproduct production before collection support is applied. Healthy mature specimens can sustain their baseline potential, while hungry, fragmented, injured, stressed, or immature specimens produce less. Immature slimes can still emit trace output, but should not perform like mature production specimens. The UI should describe this broadly as current expression, such as Dormant, Trace, Suppressed, Weak, Reduced, or Steady, without exposing exact multipliers.

Roles and intended uses do not determine natural byproducts. Using a slime as a cleaner, corpse processor, waste disposer, or production creature should not rewrite what it naturally leaves behind. Physical work may create feeding residue, contamination, waste, or transformed outputs, but that is not the same as its natural byproduct.

Collection Bay is the lab space for gathering natural byproducts without dissecting the specimen. It contains grim industrial apparatus such as drain channels, sealed troughs, fume hoods, condensers, collection plates, filters, and catch basins. The room’s apparatus works with containers rather than magically collecting everything.

Collection method should follow byproduct behavior. Dripping or liquid byproducts use drainage channels and specimen containers that can route output into collection receptacles. Sludge, gel, or sticky byproducts use troughs, catch basins, scraper plates, and lined receptacles. Vapor, haze, fume, or mist byproducts use fume hoods and condensers with existing sealed or ventable containers. Dry or particulate byproducts use plates, filters, bags, and scraper trays. Unknown or poorly understood byproducts require observation before the scientist can make reliable collection assumptions.

Specimen Drainage Tanks support drip, sludge, gel, and similar surface or runoff outputs by housing the slime in a container built around channels and catch basins. They do not solve vapor collection. Vapor-producing specimens should instead be staged in sealed or ventable containers under hood and condenser apparatus.

Collection Bay accumulation is station-based. Each staged specimen container acts as a collection station with its own active receptacle, such as a jar, flask, bag, tray, filter, or condenser bottle. Multiple slimes in the same specimen container add their natural output rate together into that station. If different natural byproducts enter the same station, collection does not pause; the station produces mixed collection residue because the scientist chose to run incompatible outputs through one apparatus path. If the active receptacle fills, output can spill into that station's apparatus overflow buffer.

Collected byproducts move into storage through station-level servicing work. Transfer requires an appropriate empty replacement vessel: the scientist physically reaches that vessel, carries it to the station, closes the station path, removes the filled receptacle, and installs the replacement. Without a reachable replacement, transfer remains blocked. Apparatus overflow is not collected during the swap; it remains in the Collection Bay and flows into the newly installed receptacle afterward. Raw byproduct names are preserved until a later processing system changes them, so smoke vapor stays smoke vapor unless it is run through a condenser or another refining procedure later. Routine transfer accounting belongs in inventory history and tooltips, while the event log should remain for meaningful observations, incidents, and discoveries.

## Research Projects and Technology Unlocks

Research is evidence-driven physical work, not an abstract point currency. Ordinary tests, harvesting, necropsy, maturation, and natural division create saved evidence records tied to their method, source, specimen, time, and confidence. Evidence can be cited by multiple projects and is never consumed. Repeating the same source cannot duplicate its record.

Each project requires an operational research-capable workbench, relevant existing scientist skill, specific observations, physical stockpile resources, harvested specimen material, and scientist labor. The scientist physically visits the reserved inputs and walks to the workstation before work begins. Only one project may be active at a time. Once physical work starts, inputs become part of a persistent project workpiece; cancellation pauses that workpiece instead of refunding incorporated materials or discarding progress. Completion is deterministic once the declared requirements and work are satisfied.

The starter research set is Controlled Stress Methodology, Longitudinal Vitality Study, Reproductive Cycle Documentation, and Reinforced Observation Vessels. Visual, Sustenance, Element, Containment, Byproduct, and Behavior tests remain basic procedures. Stress Test, Lifespan Study, and Reproduction Survey require their associated completed projects. Reinforced Observation Vessels unlocks a persistent blueprint and places one usable reinforced prototype in the Main Lab. Research uses Medicine, Husbandry, or Materials Science as appropriate; it does not create a generic Research skill. Physical diagnostic instruments, samples, calibration, and saved uncertainty-aware readings remain evidence-producing workflows rather than abstract research currency.

The Experiment Notebook organizes investigations without discovering hidden mappings for the player. A draft records a player-written hypothesis, one optional formal control, one to three experimental subjects, and an independent-variable category. Experimental slots may point to existing living creatures or to the current planned Foundry genome; unresolved planned genomes enter the ordinary synthesis and hauling flow. Starting records immutable, knowledge-safe baseline snapshots. Existing tests and diagnostics attach as cited evidence only after the experiment starts, and repeated evidence sources do not become independent observations.

A conclusion is deliberate physical work at a reachable research workbench. The player chooses Supports, Contradicts, or Inconclusive; the simulation never chooses an interpretation or reveals an undiscovered gene mapping. Completion records immutable final snapshots, known genome and observed-trait differences, condition changes, confounders, cited sources, and a deterministic confidence band. Formal controls improve confidence, while missing controls and changed conditions remain visible limitations. The completed experiment creates one reusable Research evidence record, and canceling conclusion work returns the notebook to Running without discarding its history.

## Room-Local Storage and Hauling

Physical item records are the sole source of truth for prototype resources, inventory, collected byproducts, harvested specimen materials, receptacles, residue, and waste. Lab-wide and room-local totals are derived read models rather than mutable storage ledgers. Each record stores its actual tile or container interior, room, quantity, unit mass and volume, form, tags, containing fixture or floor location, stockpile designation, carrier, and last observed quantity. Every living actor has a persistent, versioned inventory record with mass and volume capacity, indexes of carried physical stacks and durable items, and actor-neutral equipment attachment points. Those indexes and occupied slots are reconciled from physical stack ownership on load and mutation so actor inventories cannot duplicate items, equip something they do not carry, or disagree with physical storage. Scientist base capacity remains 25 kg and 80 L before worn storage bonuses; slime capacity scales from current body mass and volume with a small minimum for future assistant behavior. Physical availability depends on where the item is, whether its fixture is accessible, whether it is already carried, and whether an actor can reach an interaction point. Waste still defaults to the Pits because it is a problem to manage, not a clean supply shelf.

Room stockpile UI is framed as last-inventoried knowledge rather than perfect omniscience. A stack's actual quantity and the scientist's known quantity are separate. Inspecting the scientist's current room refreshes its stack records; unobserved consumption can leave a stale last-inventoried count until the room is checked again. The Stores workspace shows global known totals, room-local breakdowns, independent stockpile designations, and each storage fixture's known contents, access state, and capacity. Selecting a physical stack or storage fixture exposes the same information through the map inspector. Tools appear in the same physical room supply readout as materials because they are stored or carried objects, not a magical equipment cloud. Waste inside pit containers or the Pits room is displayed as pit contents rather than clean inventory.

Stockpile designations are persistent named identities independent of rooms and generic Stockpile Zone records. A designation owns accepted-category filters, DF-style priority 1-7, a tile mask, and whether floor storage is permitted. Ordinary designated floor storage is allowed by default. Storage furniture improves capacity, organization, and protection rather than creating the stockpile itself. Changing designation filters or priorities immediately creates scientist hauling work for any known misplaced stack when a valid destination exists; if no valid destination exists, the item remains physically where it is.

The starter Storage Room contains one open shelf, one closed unlocked crate, and one locked cabinet. Each has material composition, footprint, interaction points, volume capacity, mass capacity, security, access state, and individually listed contents. Shelves are always open and exposed. Crates can be opened or closed. Secure cabinets can also be locked; the scientist is authorized to use locked laboratory storage, while loose creatures are not. Starter construction materials use the shelf, biomass and feedstocks use the crate, and tools use the locking cabinet. Contextual controls can open, close, lock, and unlock storage and configure the surrounding stockpile designation.

Deleting a room designation does not delete or teleport its stockpile. Its physical stacks remain on their existing tiles and stay part of derived lab-wide totals. If another appropriate stockpile designation exists, the scientist can automatically haul them there; otherwise the supplies remain where they were.

Rooms do not have an abstract capacity limit. Storage furniture enforces its own volume and mass limits, while floor contents occupy physical tile space according to their mass, volume, and form. Each map tile represents one square meter. Actor-relative occupancy allows small creatures, people, corpses, and modest piles to share a tile while space remains; sufficiently large creatures or crowded contents prevent another actor from entering. Multi-tile and multi-layer creature bodies use Shape-derived occupied-cell masks, current dimensions, orientation, and vertical clearance.

Routine physical labor uses persistent work orders above individual scientist tasks. Current labor categories are Hauling, Cleaning, Servicing, Maintenance, and Repair. An order records its priority from 1 to 7, physical source and target, requirements, progress, claimant, and blocked reason. A scientist task is only the current actor claim on that intent. Explicit queued actions take precedence; when the scientist is idle, the highest-priority reachable permitted work order can be claimed. The scientist currently has global category permissions, while the same order structure is intended to support future workers and role-specific permissions without changing the physical work itself.

Cleanup is physical labor governed by a global policy: Manual Only, Hazardous Spills, or All Spills. Automatic cleanup applies only to observed spills that meet the policy. A cleanup order requires enough appropriate empty receptacle capacity for the entire batch; if the correct vessels are unavailable, the order remains blocked and the spill stays in place. Completing cleanup consumes those empty receptacles and produces one or more filled, tagged Waste vessels rather than deleting the material. Airborne material already released by the spill remains until it physically settles, decays, is absorbed, or is removed by a later environmental system; cleaning the source does not erase it.

Hauling preserves physical identity. Rubble is moved as rubble into an appropriate stockpile rather than becoming refined Stone Blocks, and large piles require multiple trips according to the scientist's bounded mass and volume capacity. Corpse hauling changes only the remains' physical location; it never performs necropsy, harvesting, processing, or disposal. A corpse too heavy for manual carrying remains blocked until suitable hauling equipment exists. Routine blocked work remains visible in Tasks rather than creating an incident, although hazardous consequences caused by neglected work may create incidents through their own systems.

Actions consume materials from their action room. If the required material exists elsewhere in the lab, clicking the same action button should queue a material hauling task first, then automatically queue or perform the intended action once the delivery is complete. Command and button descriptions should explain local availability, known lab totals, and the planned haul before the player clicks. Material-haul timing and movement are physical rather than an abstract per-unit delay: the scientist walks from their current tile to the source, carries each load to the destination, returns for additional loads when mass or volume requires multiple trips, and remains at the destination after delivery. Timing includes pickup/drop-off handling per carrying trip plus every walked route leg. Synthesis therefore hauls Biomass to the Synthesis Tube before starting synthesis. Manual feeding hauls feedstock to the slime's room before feeding. Auto-feeding remains local-only for now so automation does not silently move supplies through the lab.

The Resources map overlay is selected-focus only. The player chooses one material or category, such as Biomass, Waste, Tools & Supplies, Collected Byproducts, or Harvested Specimen Materials, and the map highlights rooms with known matching stock or pit contents. Stores menu rows can switch to this overlay through Show on Map actions so item browsing and map inspection stay connected. The overlay should not show every resource at once; broad all-resource highlighting would become unreadable and would blur the distinction between clean supplies, dirty pit contents, and local mess.

Collection Bay transfer is a Servicing order. The scientist routes through the physical replacement vessel and station, swaps the filled receptacle, and leaves any apparatus overflow in the Collection Bay to flow into the replacement. Collection servicing policies support Manual Only, full-receptacle, or player-set fill thresholds with a priority. Automatic servicing performs one swap by default; an explicit policy can keep replacing receptacles until overflow is cleared. The Stores Collection Stations tab remains a read-only station view, while Transfer Receptacle lives on the selected station container's contextual commands. Overflow remains apparatus contents rather than collected inventory.

The black market turns collected natural byproducts into illegal trade goods through persistent contacts, individual trust, partially known payment reliability, global reputation, and money. Low-commitment spot sales remain available for early liquidity. Formal offers instead expire harmlessly unless accepted; acceptance creates a saved contract with exact physical receptacle-content reservations, a delivery deadline, payment terms, exposure risk, and relationship consequences. The player may accept the proposed terms or make one structured counteroffer for higher payment, more time, a discreet handoff, or escrow. Counteroffer outcomes are seeded and saved rather than rerolled by reloading.

Contract delivery is not an instant "sell all" action. It queues a scientist Black Market task through every designated physical source and the Concealed Exit, accounts for carrying capacity and repeated loads, and keeps the scientist unavailable for other lab work until the trip completes. Payment may be secured in escrow, made on delivery, or delayed as risky credit; full, partial, or default outcomes are fixed when the contract is accepted but remain hidden behind contact-history descriptions. Ignored offers carry no penalty, while canceling or missing an accepted obligation reduces trust and reputation and may put the contact into a saved cooldown. Successful reputation thresholds generate persistent referrals. Observed handoffs raise Suspicion and create structured exposure records for the later investigation system. The contract data model deliberately supports future specimen commissions and illegal services, but the current physical shipment requirements are limited to collected byproducts.

Debug cheat commands can place resources and inventory into a specific room with an optional room argument. This is mainly for prototype testing and should make room-local systems easier to inspect without playing through long production chains.

## Lab Blueprint and Physical Pathfinding

The starter laboratory now has a saved physical blueprint instead of only an abstract room list. The map uses 1 meter tiles with fixed first-pass footprints for the Main Lab, Menagerie, Pits, Bedroom, Storage Room, Collection Bay, and Concealed Exit. Terrain is the spatial truth: explicitly excavated cells are walkable floor, while every other known underground tile remains solid earth unless later construction changes it. Architectural compartments are derived from that terrain, while room cell masks are persistent player designations over physical floor. A room purpose is then assigned to a designation. These layers must remain distinct: pathfinding reads terrain, architectural inference reads geometry and portals, management systems read room designations, and room purposes describe intended use.

The fixed starter laboratory is surrounded by geology generated lazily and deterministically from the run seed and cell coordinates. Limestone, granite, fractured shale, and mana-responsive basalt differ in hardness, structural stability, permeability, mana response, excavation time, tool wear, and rubble yield. Normal player knowledge reveals only broad properties visible at an exposed rock face. Mineral veins and contaminated-gas, mana-saturated, or geothermal pockets remain hidden until excavation reaches them; Debug may reveal the full generated profile. Because these features are coordinate-derived rather than rolled when mined, saving and reloading cannot reroll a discovery.

Excavation produces the actual local stone and any revealed ore as physical rubble, and opened pockets immediately affect the tile environment. The save records only sparse encounters rather than materializing the surrounding field. Laboratory expansion progress is derived from physical state through Designated, Excavated, Reachable, Cleared, Structurally Safe, Lit, Ventilated, Equipped, and Commissioned readiness stages. These are status descriptions rather than a second construction state machine: changing terrain, access, support, utilities, or fixtures changes readiness directly.

The underground map now uses sparse signed integer z-coordinates, with the starter laboratory on `z = 0`. Each z-layer represents a 4 meter vertical interval, leaving roughly 3 to 3.5 meters of ordinary usable space plus floor and ceiling structure. This is intentionally taller than a conventional building story so large creatures and equipment are not forced into implausibly shallow spaces; actors and fixtures that exceed one layer can occupy multiple vertical cells. The map camera views one layer at a time and uses `[` and `]` to move down and up. Unrevealed layers remain blank darkness during normal play, while Debug may inspect the underlying map state.

New runs begin from a saved, immutable starting-scenario record rather than one hard-coded universal setup. A scenario selects a versioned site blueprint and starting loadout profile, then materializes those definitions into ordinary authoritative rooms, terrain, doors, connectors, fixtures, containers, stockpiles, physical supplies, scientist spawn state, site identity, and explicit starting liabilities. Once play begins, the materialized state belongs to the save; later edits to its source definition do not reconstruct the site. Fixed blueprint topology remains separate from seed-driven geology, genetics, contacts, and other procedural variation. Scenario-less legacy saves receive Underground Laboratory Debug provenance without rebuilding their existing physical state.

Chemistry Front is the first normal scenario. It begins with Helix Applied Reagents as an inherited specialty-reagent identity, the divided surface shell, the surface chemistry utility/equipment line, and the established underground laboratory and loadout. Its incomplete records and undeclared research basement are saved liabilities but do not yet simulate cover credibility. The current definition and site blueprint are version three; older Chemistry Front saves retain their recorded provenance and materialized topology rather than acquiring later room or equipment changes during normalization. The Underground Laboratory scenario is available only with Debug tools and materializes the earlier underground-only topology: no surface ground, shell, chemistry fixtures, front door, or ordinary basement stair, with positive layers remaining unexcavated earth. Future business starts should extend the same data definitions instead of branching simulation rules by scenario ID.

Each site saves an explicit surface elevation instead of inferring that every positive z-coordinate is outdoors. The inherited site uses `surfaceZ = 1`, above the starter laboratory, and includes a bounded parcel of natural or gravel ground plus a roofed shell divided into Public Reception, Staff Operations, Process Hall, Hazardous Storage, Loading Bay, and a Secured Basement Vestibule. These facility classifications describe intended use; physical doors and actor access masks remain the enforcement systems. Surface ground is a first-class walkable and buildable terrain type rather than excavated rock. Areas beyond the bounded property are off-site map space rather than subterranean stone.

The Chemistry Front exposes three separate boundary endpoints. Public Entrance is a closed, unlocked, one-meter personnel door into Reception. Loading Bay is a closed, locked three-tile freight portal into the freight staging room. Concealed Exit remains a distinct covert underground fixture rather than an alternate label for either lawful entrance. Internal staff, hazardous, and basement doors carry their own access classes and emergency close-and-lock policies; lockdown still queues physical operations instead of changing them remotely. Visitors, vehicles, lawful markets, finished chemical recipes, and cover simulation remain later systems.

The inherited surface process line contains six distinct physical machines: Wet Chemistry Bench and Reaction Vessel in Process Hall, Fume Hood in Process Hall, Analysis Station in Staff Operations, Packaging Station in Loading Bay, and Waste Treatment Station in Hazardous Storage. A paired service head and riser explicitly cross the basement/surface boundary, then a walk-over surface trunk distributes air, drain, electricity, mana, and clean water. Clean water comes from a finite cistern/pump with a saved quality reading; low quality, empty storage, disabled service, broken connections, and insufficient capacity remain explainable network states.

Chemistry machines begin worn, dirty, poorly calibrated, shut down, and uncommissioned. Commissioning, startup, orderly shutdown, calibration, internal cleaning, and generic diagnostic cycles are persistent routed work rather than remote toggles. Each machine declares absolute utility requirements and lesser degradable services. Missing absolute service or critical mechanical condition blocks work; lesser deficiencies remain an explicit accepted operating risk. Test cycles deterministically combine condition, cleanliness, calibration, wear, utility deficiencies, and seeded checks to create local diagnostic waste, fumes, heat, condition loss, and eligible faults. Spills remain exact physical stacks and use the existing cleanup and vertical-fall rules. These diagnostic cycles create no saleable material; recipes, batch provenance, and legal classification belong to the following chemistry-production pass.

Building-envelope state is derived from physical topology. Open-sky ground is outdoors, roofed but unenclosed ground is covered exterior, and roofed space isolated from the outside by constructed walls and installed doorway fixtures is interior. A breached exterior doorway exposes the connected roofed space without deleting its room identity. Roofs use the same shared horizontal-boundary records as floors and ceilings, retain condition and material support behavior, and appear as a complete surface on their own z-layer while the occupied layer below remains readable. The starter roof saves an engineered truss span as part of its physical structure.

Vertical movement requires physical topology. Carved stairs connect the same `x,y` position on adjacent layers and add their full 4 meter vertical distance to travel time. Player-designed ramps rise exactly one layer and may use a custom direction, width, and run length. A four-tile run corresponds to a 45-degree grade, while longer runs are shallower. Compact one-tile ramps are intentionally allowed as a gameplay abstraction so ramps and future rails do not consume implausibly large portions of every floor; steepness instead increases movement cost and limits bulky or heavy loads. Ordinary actors may traverse a steep ramp when they physically fit, but carrying large fixtures may require a wider or longer route.

Every vertical boundary is one shared physical structure: a floor viewed from above is the ceiling viewed from below. Channeling removes that support and creates an open shaft rather than a route. Constructing a ceiling from below requires elevated-work equipment and creates the same slab record as constructing a floor above. Intact and breached slabs block movement; a breach can transmit directed attacks before the structure is destroyed enough to become an open shaft. Directed attacks, abilities, and perception use three-dimensional distance and require an open shaft, suitable breach, or physical connector rather than crossing layers abstractly.

Constructed slabs distribute support from natural rock and supported structures according to their material span. Walls, ramps, and fixtures inherit support from their physical anchors. Unsupported structures enter visible strained, failing, and collapsing states before failure; collapse destroys the physical object, leaves rubble, damages actors below when appropriate, and rechecks gravity and neighboring support. Unsupported actors, loose corpses, rubble, and loose physical stacks fall to the next supported excavated cell and can take distance-based damage. Placement previews and collision checks include every vertical cell occupied by a multi-layer fixture.

Automatic architectural inference remains layer-local, while a player may deliberately designate one room across several layers. Tile environments use the exact 3D coordinate, exchange heat and mana through solid vertical boundaries according to material properties, and exchange air, humidity, mana, heat, and exact airborne substances freely through stairs, ramps, or open shafts. First-pass liquid behavior is deliberately vertical: a newly exposed spill falls through open unsupported space and retains its exact physical identity, while horizontal spreading, pressure, and connected fluid bodies remain future work.

Automatic compartment inference recognizes broad room-like areas, narrow corridor-like runs, installed doors, and open one-tile thresholds. A single excavated tile connecting two broader spaces separates them architecturally even without a door, although movement and environmental diffusion still pass freely. Long one-tile runs are treated as corridor-like spaces rather than a chain of tiny rooms. Installed doors remain architectural portals whether open or closed, so ordinary door use never makes room identity flicker. A breached but repairable door marks adjacent compartments compromised without deleting their identities. Completely removing a door leaves ordinary floor and can change inferred geometry in a later construction pass.

Inferred compartments are conveniences, not final authority. The Rooms overlay shows inferred areas, corridor-like spaces, thresholds, compromised boundaries, existing room ownership, and the current manual room draft. Players can accept an inferred compartment, draw a new room, or edit an existing room with left-drag paint and erase brushes. Formal room tile ownership is exclusive. Redrawing preserves the room's stable identity; explicit merges require the player to choose the surviving room, while explicit deletion removes only the designation and leaves terrain, fixtures, creatures, corpses, and loose supplies on their physical tiles. Rooms may intentionally span multiple floor regions separated by independent connector tiles. Renaming changes only the room label. If a redraw leaves a designation disconnected, its ownership is preserved and flagged rather than silently deleting a side.

Room designation policy has Off, Suggest, and Automatic modes. Off leaves inferred geometry available without creating rooms. Suggest exposes the inference for confirmation. Automatic accepts unambiguous inferred areas and can extend non-manual room designations into matching unassigned floor. It never changes manually drawn boundaries. Room designation and purpose classification are separate policies because recognizing a physical compartment is not the same decision as deciding what it is for.

Every designated room has a stable identity, an independent player-editable name, and one primary purpose. Changing a purpose never renames the room. Current purposes include Unassigned, Workroom, Containment Room, Corpse Processing Room, Quarters, Storage Room, Collection Room, Corridor, and Access Room. Purpose classification also has Off, Suggest, and Automatic modes. Automatic classification considers unambiguous physical evidence and declared zone intent, records its rationale, and may change only rooms whose purpose is Unassigned or already automatic. Starter and manually chosen purposes are protected. Ambiguous evidence leaves the purpose unchanged.

Tile-level zones layer player intent beneath the room's primary purpose and may overlap. Current zones are Work Area, Containment Zone, Stockpile Zone, Corpse Staging, Collection Zone, Rest Area, and Traffic Zone. A zone does not create equipment, capacity, safety, or capability by itself. It says how the player intends those tiles to be used, provides destinations for later hauling and work systems, and may support an otherwise physically equipped room. Zones are clipped to their room's owned tiles when boundaries change and transfer into the chosen surviving room during a merge.

Room function is evaluated rather than assumed from its label. Planned means essential physical requirements are still missing. Usable means the purpose's current essentials are met without known problems. Impaired means the room can function but has a noncritical deficiency such as a missing supporting zone, disconnected designation, straddling fixture, or elevated contamination. Unsafe means known contamination or serious incidents make use dangerous, and this warning outranks missing equipment. Requirements are deliberately physical: workrooms need laboratory equipment, containment rooms need containment fixtures, processing rooms need pits or equivalent equipment, storage needs stockpile tiles, collection needs an operational collection apparatus, access rooms need an operational exit, and quarters need an operational bed. A fixture only satisfies room function when it is intact, installed inside the room, and has at least one currently accessible interaction point.

Starter rooms are separated by continuous one-tile bands of solid rock. Each installed door is a fixture occupying exactly one excavated floor tile in a deliberately dug one-tile chokepoint. The door is not an abstract edge between two room cells and does not occupy a special wall tile. Removing it would leave ordinary walkable floor; widening the passage would allow actors and environmental flow to bypass a single door. Door state affects passage through its physical tile, while room identity remains an independent designation and does not merge when a door opens.

The simulation clock is seconds-based internally. UI speed controls offer 1x real time by default, plus restrained 5x and 10x acceleration. Hands-on scientist actions such as synthesis, testing, handling, examination, collection, and maintenance are authored in seconds so they remain playable at 1x. Rest, biology, decay, production, travel, and other genuinely extended processes retain their physical duration units, with explicit manual, next-event, or next-queue skip controls available for long waits. Task `createdAt`, `dueAt`, lifespans, decay windows, movement, and passive updates are all stored in seconds.

The blueprint is a foundation for later construction rather than a full base-builder. Scientist movement, container hauling, and material hauling store room routes plus physical map paths in queued task data. Scientist movement uses a stored movement speed in meters per second, while creature movement profiles derive both a movement style and a physical speed from shape, body consistency, appendages, element, and weight.

Navigation is a simulation service rather than a rendering behavior. A renderer-neutral module provides cardinal weighted A* planning, footprint and orientation checks, bounded route caching, revision-aware validation, and short-horizon movement reservations. Terrain, fixture, door, and access-policy changes advance saved navigation revisions and invalidate cached assumptions. Routes are also validated as actors traverse them, so a newly blocked tile or closed door cannot be crossed merely because an older path still exists.

Movement advances one segment at a time and actors physically occupy intermediate tiles. Small actors may share a one-square-meter tile while their combined floor load remains within capacity; multi-tile bodies reserve and validate their entire oriented footprint and can rotate only where the resulting footprint fits. Reservations are short-lived rather than claiming whole routes. Conflicts resolve deterministically by urgency: fleeing outranks combat, direct scientist orders outrank ordinary needs, and wandering yields first. Blocked actors wait, expose congestion in Debug, and replan after repeated delay instead of overlapping or teleporting.

Navigation obeys actor capabilities and knowledge. The scientist can spend physical interaction time opening an unlocked closed door while traversing a route; ordinary slimes require an already open or breached passage. Blind slimes plan from local perception and remembered route cells rather than receiving omniscient access to the full map. Debug navigation diagnostics expose revisions, planner/cache statistics, actor footprints, movement records, and active reservations without granting that information to normal play.

Doors are physical constructed objects rather than simple open/closed markers. Each map door has a stable fixture ID and stores one or more contiguous floor cells plus a base construction type, clearance, material, condition, seal strength, lock strength, access requirement, lockdown order, and optional wards using the same broad durability language as containers. A multi-cell portal is still one logical fixture with one condition and security state. Adjacent room IDs are derived from the current tile designations, so redraws, merges, deletions, and renames do not replace the door or reset its condition, wards, access settings, lock, seal, or breach state. A door with an undesignated side remains a controllable physical fixture. Current door states include open, closed, locked, sealed, and breached. Ordinary closed unlocked doors block uncontrolled creature movement but can be used by authorized scientist movement, hauling, and material hauling according to door policy. Locked and sealed doors block routing until physically changed, while breached doors no longer lock or seal and can be passed through. Door authorization never substitutes for physical state: an unauthorized actor can physically cross an already-open door if forced, a permitted actor still needs a usable route and the capability to operate it, and an administrative rule does not stop a loose slime.

Payload clearance is part of physical route validation. Carried loads record mass, volume, width, length, and height; doors reject loads beyond their opening and vertical connectors apply their own handling limits. The ordinary carved stair permits scientist hand loads but rejects bulky containers, freight volumes, overwide objects, and loads that require a ramp or hoist. The Loading Bay freight portal accepts substantially wider and taller cargo without making that cargo magically compatible with the stair. Container and material-haul planning uses the same payload checks, and queued work revalidates them when topology or door state changes.

The Stores workspace can queue an exact quantity of one physical resource between two selected rooms. Carry capacity divides the request into explicit repeated haul legs. Pickup converts the source stack into a carried physical stack, delivery materializes it at the destination, and already delivered legs remain delivered. If a door locks or a route becomes unusable, the task pauses with its current load still in the scientist's inventory; save and reload preserve that state. Cancellation drops only the current carried load on the scientist's actual tile, while source material not yet picked up and material delivered on earlier trips stay where they physically are.

Named access areas are tile masks independent of room ownership and purpose. Allowed areas constrain routine autonomous work to their union, while forbidden areas override allowed areas on overlapping tiles. Areas can be assigned through reusable actor access profiles and can apply routinely or only while emergency restrictions are active. The first profile belongs to the scientist, but the saved model supports later intelligent workers and individual assignments. Direct scientist movement may override a current restriction after an explicit warning; that override records only the exact restricted tiles and door requirements the player confirmed. A different restriction added while the task is queued still blocks it. Routine queued work does not ask for permission and instead reports the access reason that blocks it. Ordinary loose slimes ignore all such administrative policy and continue to obey only physical barriers, senses, biology, and abilities. Future repellents such as Slime Bane should therefore be physical magical fields or wards rather than access settings.

Door behavior policy describes what an actor does after using an ordinary door: restore its prior state, leave it open, or close it after passage. It does not give the door an automatic motor. Direct open, close, lock, unlock, seal, and unseal commands are queued scientist tasks that require travel to an adjacent operating tile and physical interaction time. Each door also has an access class and a configured emergency response. Activating emergency restrictions enables emergency-only access areas and queues the necessary door-operation tasks; it does not teleport, remotely change, or automatically restore ordinary doors. True automatic or remote doors will require appropriate machinery, power, enchantment, or another explicit physical capability.

Released slimes now act as loose map actors. They are not added to the normal task queue; instead, each loose slime can choose a simple creature activity such as wandering, seeking accessible mess, moving toward contamination, feeding on room residue, feeding on loose waste, feeding on accessible remains, feeding on exposed physical resource stacks, or seeking a better adjacent habitat. They use exact local food targets only when the food is in the same room. Adjacent-room residue, loose waste, exposed remains, sought contamination, or better environmental conditions can create qualitative traces even through closed doors, with trace strength reduced by door state and seal quality. A trace tells the slime that something relevant is nearby, not the exact item, inventory stack, or hidden formula. Slimes can only move toward trace sources if an open or breached route exists; closed, locked, and sealed doors block them, causing blocked-door pressure and possible-intent observations instead. In-flight slime routes are checked against the current door state, so a door closing ahead of a moving slime stops the route rather than letting it pass through. Floor stock and open shelves are accessible food; closed crates and locked cabinets protect their contents.

Map-based incident alerts are now a lightweight spatial emergency layer. Unresolved alerts can be created for observed loose creatures, blocked-door pressure, notable room contamination, feeding residue spills, exposed remains, corpse overflow, compromised or breached containers, breached doors, and observed combat. Alerts store type, severity, source, room, map cell, status, acknowledgement timing, response movement timing, stale timing, manual resolution timing, and urgency timing. The Incident Alerts panel is the always-visible pressure surface; map markers appear through the Incidents overlay or when a specific incident is selected, keeping the blueprint from permanently shouting every known problem. Multiple incidents on one tile stack into one marker with a count, acknowledged incidents become visually quieter, and stale incidents remain as last-known locations until the player checks or clears them. The player can acknowledge an incident, focus its map source, queue a scientist movement task to the alert site, initiate emergency restrictions, or manually mark the alert resolved. Manual resolution suppresses that exact observation until it materially changes. New serious non-combat incidents reduce time to 1x, and new critical non-combat incidents pause at 1x when discovered.

Recapture is a physical compound scientist task rather than an instant containment toggle. It requires a currently exact observation of the loose creature and an explicitly selected empty, usable destination container. The task records a route to an adjacent handling tile and onward to the destination. If the creature moves before the scientist reaches it, or becomes only a stale or uncertain observation, the task blocks at the last-known plan instead of tracking it omnisciently. Once the scientist reaches and secures the creature, portable-containment recapture physically carries the slime with the scientist, while pit recapture represents the scientist herding or luring it along the same physical route. Canceling the order releases it at the scientist's current tile. Actively fighting or attacking creatures must first be subdued, trapped, or distracted.

Recapture risk uses actual creature biology, stress, current mass, container compatibility, the scientist's Creature Handling skill, and the configured physical handling method. Normal UI exposes only broad Routine, Risky, or Extremely Dangerous assessments from discovered factors and clearly lists unknown factors. Poor-but-possible attempts remain available. Failure can injure the scientist, stress the slime, wear the handling tool, and leave the creature loose at the failure location. Success moves the creature into the chosen physical container, closes its ordinary access where possible, applies local fouling and tool wear, and resolves the loose-creature condition through the ordinary incident refresh. Breached containers are invalid destinations; compromised but still functional containers remain risky choices. Repair and tactical subdual remain separate passes.

Baiting is also physical. A Place Bait tile command reserves one real feedstock unit, queues scientist travel to retrieve it, and leaves that unit exposed on the selected floor tile. The bait immediately creates an appropriate chemical trace and then participates in the existing loose-slime feeding, sensory-gradient, consumption, and residue systems. It does not compel a creature, reveal a hidden creature, or guarantee that the creature values that feedstock. Emergency lockdown remains a manual command that enables emergency access restrictions and queues each configured physical door operation; ordinary doors never move remotely without appropriate future machinery or magic.

Environmental state is authoritative at the tile level rather than on room labels. Each underground air tile and bounded surface-ground tile stores temperature in degrees Celsius, relative humidity, ambient mana density in thaums/m³, and a sparse mixture of exact airborne substance identities and concentrations. Generic Contamination remains only a player-facing aggregate of that mixture. Room and inferred-compartment values are derived weighted summaries, so renaming, splitting, merging, or deleting room designations cannot move air or erase hazards.

Open adjacent tiles exchange heat, humidity, mana, and each airborne substance independently. Exact airborne identities are conserved through internal diffusion and persist unless a physical process settles, decays, absorbs, vents, or removes them. Outdoor and roof tiles exchange with an exterior atmospheric reservoir: temperature, humidity, and mana relax toward ambient conditions while named airborne substances disperse without first becoming anonymous Contamination. Doors modify exchange according to state, condition, material, effective seal quality, and relevant wards. Natural rock is airtight but conducts heat and mana. Constructed barriers can slowly leak air and humidity according to material porosity and damage, while thermal resistance and arcane permeability govern heat and mana transfer. A breached wall is not passable until destroyed, but attacks and environmental transfer may pass through the breach according to its condition.

Container interiors remain separate environments and exchange only with the exact tile at their physical access location. Geometry supplies interior volume; open state, material porosity, seal quality, condition, and wards determine the transfer rate. Temperature and mana exchange according to material properties, while airborne substances preserve their identities across the boundary. Ordinary UI reports descriptive environmental bands; exact measurements and substance identities require appropriate future instruments or Debug inspection. Light is a tile field produced by physical fixtures and the scientist's saved carried hand lamp; electrical charge remains a room-era summary until electrical hazards and stored charge receive their own physical model.

Environmental infrastructure is physical rather than a room-wide toggle. Air ducts, drains, electrical conduits, and mana conduits form separate networks only when their fixture footprints touch or are cardinally adjacent. Devices may share a map tile when they occupy different physical layers, but deconstruction targets the selected fixture's stable ID. Each connected component reports source, conduit, demand, reserve, utilization, and redundancy. Finite conduit throughput caps usable capacity; when demand exceeds it, stable player-set priorities allocate service before fixture-ID order breaks ties. Multiple compatible sources provide automatic failover without erasing the physical bottleneck between source and load.

Ordinary powered devices can use local fuel, connected electricity, or connected stored mana when their definition supports that source. Fuel-burning devices consume physical Fuel Reagent and add heat and fuel smoke to their own tile or the adjacent room air when wall-mounted. Heat output therefore remains local even when its electricity or mana arrived through a network, and fuel remains finite at the device or generator that burns it. The starter laboratory uses independent primitive devices rather than a central utility plant; most active climate and processing devices begin switched off, while an oil lamp provides initial local light.

Lighting propagates from active fixtures and the scientist's carried hand lamp across physical air tiles with distance falloff. Open-sky tiles also receive a deterministic day/night daylight curve and propagate that neutral daylight through physical openings; weather, seasons, wind, precipitation, and windows remain deferred. Solid rock, constructed walls, roofs, and closed intact doors block light. Open stairs, ramps, and shafts pass light vertically with additional falloff. The scientist has no intrinsic glow: the starter hand lamp is saved physical equipment with condition, enabled state, intensity, radius, and a semantic warm spectrum. Heating changes the heater's exact tile and then spreads through ordinary environmental exchange. Heaters expose broad Cold, Comfortable, and Warm controls until an operational thermostat in the same room permits an exact Celsius target.

Ventilation requires a physically connected air terminal and powered fan. Filters have finite capacity and preserve every captured airborne identity; an exterior terminal can discharge the uncaptured remainder. Supply mode exchanges temperature and humidity with outside air, while exhaust mode removes the exact local air mixture. Hazardous exterior exhaust is not guaranteed to increase Suspicion, but accumulated discharge creates deterministic opportunities for authorities to observe evidence.

Drains act only on spills physically occupying the drain tile and connected apparatus until a later liquid-flow simulation exists. Drainpipes route exact tagged contents into finite sumps; a full or disconnected sump blocks further collection unless an exterior outfall exists. Exterior liquid discharge can create Suspicion through the same evidence-risk principle as ventilation. Emptying a filter or sump creates physical tagged waste rather than deleting its contents.

Mana collectors have finite storage and can either extract mana slowly from the surrounding rock reservoir or consume loaded Arcane Feedstock. Connected mana emitters spend that stored mana to raise the density on their exact tile toward a configured target. Mana, heat, light, airborne matter, and drained waste therefore remain physical quantities or fields whose location and network access matter.

Utility fixtures accumulate saved runtime, service hours, and wear while operating; highly utilized components wear faster. Seeded failure checks occur at stable wear milestones, so advancing time in different chunk sizes does not reroll outcomes. Faults may impair output or sever a conduit/source. Their symptoms are visible immediately, but the exact fault remains unknown until a routed inspection completes. Diagnosed faults create a distinct physical repair procedure using Metal Parts and, for air or drainage networks, replacement seals. Preventive maintenance reduces wear, resets the service clock, and modestly restores condition without pretending to repair an active fault. Each fixture can use a 24-, 72-, or 168-service-hour schedule or remain manual-only; scheduled work enters the same persistent priority and labor-permission system as other maintenance.

Slime habitat response now uses those room and container environment values. Bad habitat gently raises Stress over time, while truly Hostile habitat can reduce natural byproduct expression. Good habitat can slowly calm a stressed slime. Hostile habitat adds a small active containment pressure; Poor Fit becomes a containment issue mainly after Stress has actually built up. This keeps habitat meaningful without creating an instant fail state before the player has full infrastructure controls.

Map object placement is now saved instead of inferred only for display. Containers occupy blocking footprints based on their physical dimensions, with sub-meter equipment rounded up to one 1-meter tile and larger round or pit-like containers occupying square bounds around their diameter. Contained creatures and contained remains do not get separate map cells; the container owns the physical footprint. Loose actors use floor-load capacity and complete physical footprints to determine whether they may share or enter a tile.

Creature bodies are physical volumes rather than anchor points. Current mass and generated dimensions determine horizontal span and vertical layer clearance, while Shape determines a renderer-neutral occupied-cell mask inside those bounds. Long bodies rotate during pathfinding and turning consumes time. Small one-cell creatures can share floor area; multi-cell bodies reserve every occupied cell. Growth may rotate a body to use available space, but cannot expand through rock, structures, or other occupants. Blocked growth and undersized containment create saved spatial pressure and Stress, while a specimen with no valid release space remains trapped instead of clipping into the map. Loose corpses retain the mass, orientation, shape mask, and vertical extent the creature had at death, then shrink physically as they are consumed. Feeding, contact, combat distance, hauling access, map selection, and occupancy all use these body volumes. Unobserved creatures project their last-known body footprint and location to the ordinary UI; only Debug may expose current hidden geometry.

Pathfinding treats container and blocking fixture footprints as blocked cells and routes controlled movement to usable access cells rather than onto the object tile. Costs account for terrain, doors, and congestion rather than treating every traversable cell as identical. Container hauling records the source footprint, destination footprint, adjacent access cells, and physical path. Furniture and laboratory fixtures can now be placed manually through construction, while starter containers remain automatically placed and fine-grained room capacity can come later.

The blueprint is inspectable in the first pass. Hovering a tile reports its room, door, object, blocking, and route context. Clicking map cells now selects into the map-side Selection inspector rather than immediately pulling the player into a legacy list panel. Stacked-cell click priority should surface the entity most likely to matter to the player: incidents and the scientist still outrank ordinary objects, occupied container footprints can select their contained slime or contained remains before the container itself, and empty containers remain selectable as physical objects. The map highlights the selected target and only the next queued movement or hauling path to avoid visual clutter.

Construction begins from the physical blueprint through a shared designation workflow. Current map modes are Mine, Smooth Floor, Smooth Wall, Build, Deconstruct, and Cancel Orders. Left-click and drag paint the active draft, the draft can be cleared without affecting confirmed work, and confirmation creates a persistent construction order at a Dwarf Fortress-style priority from 1 to 7, where 1 is highest. Walls, floors, doors, and fixtures all use the same order and scientist-task layers even though their recipes and physical placement rules differ.

Construction orders and scientist tasks are separate layers. An order stores its intended transformation, priority, creation time, and per-tile Planned, Queued, Completed, or Canceled state. The automatic work claimant chooses the highest-priority reachable tile, puts that one tile into the scientist queue, and claims another only after the current construction task finishes. This is the first foundation for later RimWorld-style actor work permissions: designations express player intent, eligible actors decide whether they may perform that work, and a task represents one actor's current claim. The scientist physically routes to a work position and excavation changes one tile at a time, so newly opened floor can make deeper mining orders reachable. Physically valid but unreachable plans are not rejected; they remain blocked indefinitely with a specific reason until topology changes or the player cancels them. A blocked high-priority tile does not prevent a reachable lower-priority tile from being claimed.

The physical tile model now separates excavated base terrain, floor surfaces, constructed structures, door fixtures, and loose rubble. A constructed wall occupies previously excavated floor and blocks movement without erasing the floor beneath it; deconstructing the wall exposes unassigned floor again. Constructed stone flooring overlays excavated floor and removes the rough-floor movement penalty. Doors remain independent stable-ID fixtures on excavated floor. A new door must have at least one cardinally adjacent natural or constructed wall to anchor its frame, but the game does not prevent ineffective placement or construction that strands the scientist. Current first-pass recipes are stone-block walls, stone floors, and rough wood doors. Construction materials are reserved only when a reachable scientist task claims a tile, and cancellation returns reserved material.

Natural solid rock has an implicit stone composition with sparse saved ore-bearing deposits. Mining creates local physical rubble rather than immediately adding refined resources to inventory: ordinary rock yields stone rubble, while ore-bearing rock yields both stone and ore fragments. Deconstructing walls, floors, and doors likewise leaves material-tagged rubble on the affected tile. Rubble is visible and persistent and can be hauled to compatible stockpile tiles without refining it. Processing, detailed stacking, obstruction, and advanced stockpile acceptance remain later work.

Excavation, smoothing, construction, and deconstruction require persistent physical tools rather than an assumed kit. The starter lab includes one mining pick, masonry hammer, stone chisel, wood axe, hand saw, pry bar, and shovel for prototype testing. Tasks request capabilities such as excavation, stone shaping, controlled striking, masonry, woodworking, wood cutting, masonry breaking, or prying rather than hardcoded item names. A tool must meet a minimum capability against the target material: an inadequate shovel cannot mine solid rock, while a poor but valid tool works slowly and wears faster. Multi-tool work such as smoothing requires both a chisel and striking tool, and the scientist can carry at most a primary and secondary construction tool.

Construction tools are individual objects with material composition, craftsmanship quality, condition, maximum durability, location, carrier, reservation, and capabilities. Their physical stack record now leaves its shelf or cabinet when the scientist picks the tool up and returns to a valid local stockpile when released. A claimed order routes through required tool locations and material stockpiles before reaching its work position. Tools are reserved during the task and become carried when physical work begins. The scientist retains tools while matching designated work remains, then leaves them in the current room when changing roles. Future actor work assignments should make this occupational retention explicit rather than inferred.

Tile work accumulates progressively and survives interruption. Tool wear occurs as work advances, based on capability, quality, condition, and target material. If a tool breaks, the task stops, the tile returns to its designation with completed work intact, and the current block and historical interruption reason remain separately visible. Basic maintenance can clean, sharpen, tighten, or adjust a usable tool to ninety percent condition without materials. Basic repair can restore broken or damaged tools and consumes compatible supplies, including Metal Parts for metal tools; complete fabrication, advanced repair, drills, explosives, and dedicated workshops remain future systems. Debug inventory commands can add, damage, restore, or change the quality of tools for testing.

Physical structures and equipment now share one material model. Stone, granite, limestone, brick, wood, iron, steel, glass, reinforced glass, ceramic, dirt, rubber, cloth, and warded composite define exact internal properties including density, hardness, toughness, porosity, flammability, thermal and corrosion resistance, conductivity, moisture resistance, arcane permeability, contamination retention, and sound transmission. Ordinary player-facing readouts translate those values into descriptive bands; debug inspection retains the exact numbers needed for testing. Walls, floors, doors, containers, and handling tools use the same derived damage-resistance vocabulary instead of maintaining unrelated resistance tables.

A physical object's composition can include a primary material plus reinforcement, lining, coating, and seal layers. Form, geometry, condition, wards, and enchantments remain distinct from composition and can modify how those materials perform. Damage is typed, reduced by the shared material profile, and applied to persistent condition. Constructed walls and natural rock become Breached below one-quarter condition: they still block movement, but attacks and directed abilities can pass through. At zero condition they are destroyed and leave material-appropriate rubble; ore-bearing natural rock also leaves its ore. Doors retain opening-like breach behavior, while damaged structural walls create spatial incidents the scientist can focus directly. Basic Repair orders restore condition using compatible physical supplies, while restoring a compromised seal is a separate order that consumes sealing material and leaves ordinary access security for a later action. Replacement, player-applied layers, advanced workshops, and enchantment workflows remain future systems.

Furniture and laboratory equipment use a shared physical fixture model. Each fixture has a stable ID, type, origin, rotation, rotated multi-tile footprint, collision layer, material composition, condition, wards, capabilities, and named interaction ports. Interaction ports rotate with the object and identify where an actor must stand to operate, maintain, load, unload, or install it. Placement validates the entire footprint against terrain, structures, doors, containers, and other fixtures on the same collision layer. Wall-mounted fixtures require physical support; floor drains and exits can remain walkable; ordinary furniture blocks its occupied tiles. The map, Selection inspector, pathfinding, structural damage, room evaluation, construction, and deconstruction all read this same saved fixture state.

The starter lab includes a physical workbench, bed, collection apparatus, storage furniture, wall lamp, heater, thermostat, ventilation line, finite air filter, drain and sump line, primitive fuel generator, electrical conduit, mana collector and emitter, and Concealed Exit for testing. These fixture families are buildable through the same material-aware construction and production workflow. The player can choose an exact material variant, the best known available material, or the closest available material, and can rotate a placement before confirmation. Inaccessible placement is legal because designations express intent rather than guaranteeing wisdom; the fixture remains impaired or unusable until at least one interaction port can be reached. Heavy site-built equipment cannot be hauled as an ordinary object. Moving it will require dismantling and reconstruction, while small portable furnishings can eventually be carried as finished items.

Fixture assembly is divided into portable finished items, workstation-built components assembled on site, and very large equipment built directly on site. The Basic Workbench is hand-assembled on site to bootstrap production. Other current fixtures and receptacles have known prototype recipes that require a compatible physical workstation, tools, material inputs, actor time, and Fabrication practice. Global bills choose a reachable compatible workstation when the scientist claims the work; workstation bills remain pinned to the selected fixture. Bills support make once, make a quantity, repeat indefinitely, and maintain-stock modes, priorities from 1 to 7, and closest-valid, best-known, exact, or allowed-material rules without granting hidden material knowledge.

Production reserves physical inputs and tools before routing the scientist to the chosen workstation. The workpiece becomes a physical workstation record only when work actually begins, gains progress over time, and survives interruption or a paused bill. Canceling started work leaves tagged crafting scrap on the workstation tile instead of refunding a finished object. Completion creates a physical output with its material composition and craftsmanship quality preserved; portable outputs can be hauled into compatible stockpiles, while fixture components remain linked to their placement dependency and are hauled to the build site before installation. Maintain-stock counts only usable empty receptacles and uncommitted finished stock, so filled vessels and items already claimed for installation do not satisfy the target. Glass, Cloth, and Rubber are now physical starter materials alongside the earlier construction resources so the initial receptacle recipes can be tested. A placement that lacks a required finished item or component automatically creates a linked production bill, and the normal scientist construction task claims the placement after that output exists. Containers and the Synthesis Tube still use specialized records but already follow the same physical ideas of footprint, access, material condition, and non-magical movement; they should migrate through adapters as the shared equipment system expands.

Production recipes now form explicit fixed-batch chains. The first representative chains turn hauled stone rubble into stone blocks and masonry components, lumber into cut boards and wooden components, and glass and rubber into glassware components, seals, and finished receptacles. Each stage creates a separate physical workpiece and ordinary stock output; sawdust, stone chips, glass fragments, and rubber offcuts remain typed physical byproducts instead of collapsing into generic Waste. A bill that lacks a known intermediate creates bounded prerequisite bills, including nested prerequisites, but reserves only the inputs for the stage the scientist is actively claiming. Pausing a parent bill pauses its unfinished generated dependencies and resuming restores only those paused by the parent; canceling the parent cancels them.

Input craftsmanship now propagates through the chain. Output quality combines the weighted input quality, the poorest critical input, Fabrication skill, tool quality, workstation condition, and a seeded bounded variation; the poorest critical input caps the result so repeated processing cannot turn crude material into a masterwork by luck alone. Maintain-stock bills may set a minimum craftsmanship floor and continue to exclude filled, reserved, or otherwise unavailable stock. The Production recipe view groups recipes by chain, identifies stage order, fixed batch yield, physical byproducts, and known prerequisite recipes, while bill and workpiece records expose generated stages, blocked reasons, inherited input quality, and produced byproduct stacks.

Hauling rubble into a stockpile now adapts terrain rubble into cataloged physical raw-material stacks without refining it. Stone rubble can feed the manual masonry chain immediately; ore fragments, wood debris, and metal scrap remain distinct raw stocks for later powered processing and recycling. The Basic Workbench provides manual stoneworking, woodworking, and glassworking bootstrap capabilities. Kilns, smelters, powered machines, and recycling remain future production extensions; they should consume the implemented finite utility capacity, fuel, reliability, and maintenance model.

Mining transforms natural solid rock into rough excavated floor and does not directly manufacture a room record or door. When the final tile in a mining order completes, compartment inference recalculates and room designation policy may accept the resulting space. Smoothing is also tile-based. Selecting rough floor creates a Smooth Floor order for that floor tile; selecting exposed solid rock creates a Smooth Wall order for that natural wall tile. Smoothed floors and smoothed walls are stored separately as physical surface state. Cancel Orders removes only pending work on painted tiles and never reverses completed terrain, room ownership, room purpose, or zones. Selecting rough floor or a room still exposes inferred acceptance, manual room drawing, room-purpose commands, and the Rooms overlay. This preserves the Dwarf Fortress-style order: dig first, recognize or draw room ownership, construct boundaries and fixtures, then define and support the room's purpose.

Future construction, room expansion, damage, sealing, and creature movement should extend this physical map and work-order model. Detailed room interaction starts from map selection and contextual commands rather than always-visible room cards; room stockpiles, station records, policies, tasks, and historical logs live in their management menus. Construction now records physical excavation and deconstruction rubble, supports basic rubble hauling and material-aware repair, services captured utility contents into physical waste, and routes utility inspection, preventive maintenance, and repair through persistent work orders. It does not yet model ore smelting, powered processing, noise, secrecy, additional workers, structural supports, or full liquid flow; those should layer onto persistent orders and physical terrain rather than replacing them.

## Frontend Architecture Direction

Helix Heresy should move toward a map-first management interface similar to Dwarf Fortress. The physical lab blueprint should become the main surface for understanding the lab, inspecting rooms and objects, issuing contextual commands, following movement, reading incidents, and eventually managing combat or larger-scale emergencies. The existing panel-heavy prototype UI is useful scaffolding, but the long-term interface should feel more like a grim laboratory command console than a collection of disconnected web panels.

The full-screen map shell now treats the blueprint as the base play surface. The map remains mounted behind the interface, the HUD keeps only compact run-state information such as time, speed, pause state, and Suspicion, and storage or waste capacity lives in management views rather than the always-visible status strip. The Map workspace itself is now intentionally lean: it shows the blueprint, Selection inspector, contextual command surface, overlay controls, and small feed, while old always-on room cards, incident panels, and construction panels have been retired from the map surface. Existing prototype screens are still reachable through the categorized workspace menu, but non-map screens open as temporary management overlays instead of permanent side panels. Time continues to run while those menus are open unless the game is already paused. This is still a transitional shell: legacy management screens will keep being narrowed into focused records, filters, settings, and map-driven commands in later passes.

The screen-manager pass stores the active workspace in `state.ui.activeWorkspaceTab` and treats the map as the home surface. Management navigation now lives in a bottom category bar closer to Dwarf Fortress or RimWorld: Lab contains Map and Foundry, Work contains Tasks, Creatures contains creature and container records, Stores contains Resources, Economy contains Black Market, Policy contains Policies, Records contains Journal and Messages, and Debug contains Cheats when prototype debug is enabled. Each category exposes its current screen tabs inside the bottom bar, while major non-map screens open as management overlays above the map. Clicking an already-open non-map tab returns to Map, and Escape still backs out in a predictable order: close keyboard help, exit command mode or command menu, return from management screens to Map, then clear the current map selection. The old queue drawer has been retired in favor of a full Tasks workspace for scientist-directed work, while the old split Scientist/Inventory resources view has been consolidated into a tabbed Stores workspace. Policies now have internal tabs for Overview, Handling, Corpses, Doors, Rooms, Feeding, and Automation rather than one growing settings pile, and the Debug workspace has separate Cheats and AI Debug tabs. The temporary Scientist tab remains there until actor/scientist management gets its own final home. Debug tools are gated behind a Debug toggle that defaults on for prototype testing; turning it off hides Cheats and removes the omniscient Debug map overlay from normal navigation.

UI state is now split by responsibility. Run-local UI state can live in `state.ui` when it is tied to the current lab, such as the active workspace, map cursor, selected overlay, selected resource overlay, message filter, and inspector tab. Imported saves open on the Map with no selection, no active overlay, the default resource focus, all-message filtering, navigation mode, and keyboard help closed so another run's viewing context does not leak into the player's current session. Local autosave loading may preserve run-local context because it is resuming the same browser run rather than accepting someone else's exported view state. Player interface preferences use a separate localStorage key rather than the save payload; the first preference-backed behavior is compact message feed visibility and limit, with room for future panel sizes, keybindings, density, and accessibility settings. Debug visibility is session-only and defaults on every browser launch during prototype testing, so exported saves do not carry a debug-on or debug-off flag. The top bar includes a Reset UI control that restores default preferences, returns the current view to Map, clears selection, and turns prototype debug tools back on.

The message system now separates urgent recent information from the full observed record. `state.events` stores typed message records with category, severity, compact-feed eligibility, and optional map links. The compact translucent feed shows recent important observed messages such as combat, incidents, critical warnings, and blocked or failed actions, while routine task completions, discoveries, resource accounting, and specimen observations stay out of the compact feed. By default the compact feed gently fades after a short period so it does not become a permanent panel, but the Messages workspace includes a UI preference to keep it persistent. The Messages workspace tab is the full history surface with category filtering and focus controls for linked known incidents, rooms, or tiles. The Journal remains the scientific reference surface for discovered traits and notes, and routine inventory/resource accounting should continue to live in inventory histories and tooltips unless it becomes unusual, blocked, dangerous, or explicitly noteworthy.

Selection now has a single forward-facing state object that can represent tiles, rooms, doors, containers, living specimens, corpses, incidents, tasks, tools, or resources. The older `selectedSlimeId` and `selectedMapTarget` values are kept as compatibility mirrors for remaining transitional records. Map clicks open a compact bottom-left Selection inspector that gives the first useful readout without pulling the player away from the blueprint. The compact inspector can expand into a larger floating window while time keeps running, exposing Details, Actions, Related, and History tabs for deeper inspection and commands. Escape collapses the expanded window before leaving the Map or clearing selection. Map clicks use top-priority selection for stacked cells; the Related tab and compact same-location links expose containers, contained specimens, contained remains, rooms, and other same-location entities without interrupting normal clicks. Container and specimen selections include an interior/context section once expanded: selected containers list living occupants, remains, containment summary, residue, pit contents, and Collection Bay station state where relevant; selected contained slimes show their actual container, physical fit, compatibility, active risk, residue, and other occupants. If a selected slime dies and creates a corpse, selection follows the corpse automatically.

The contextual command system now uses a separate floating command menu driven by the current map selection. The Selection inspector answers "what is this?", while the command menu answers "what can I do with it?" The inspector's Actions controls open this command surface without turning Actions into another detail tab. Commands are grouped by purpose, available commands appear before blocked commands within each group, and disabled commands stay visible with specific reasons. Numbered command mode still exists internally as a future/reserved interaction path, but the current player-facing map controls do not bind a keyboard key to opening contextual commands because `WASD` is reserved for map panning. Escape closes the command menu before collapsing inspector detail or leaving the map. Current first-pass commands cover selected room or tile movement, solid-earth dig drafting and confirmation, door open/close/lock/seal controls, incident focus/response/acknowledge actions, corpse necropsy/forensic/harvest/dump actions, slime Analyze/feeding/handling/role/combat/living-harvest actions, and container access/hauling/Collection Bay staging/receptacle transfer/remains/transfer actions. Remaining management screens should trend read-only unless they are filters, settings, or record navigation; new player actions should flow through reusable command functions so hotkeys, command modes, inspectors, and future map workflows have a shared foundation.

The first task queue and path visualization pass defines the normal queue as scientist-directed work only. Passive systems, creature autonomy, maturation, and facility background behavior remain simulation state or activity readouts rather than player orders. The old queue drawer has been replaced by a Tasks workspace with Queue, Blocked, and Completed tabs. Queue rows are selectable task entities, task selections use the same inspector tabs as rooms and specimens, and task rows expose Focus, Finish, and Cancel controls when appropriate. Compound material-haul orders show their continuation steps so one click can still mean "haul the materials, then do the real work" without hiding what the scientist is doing. Task summaries show status bands such as Active, Queued, Ready, or Blocked; blocked tasks do not pause the game, but they remain visible with a reason until the obstruction is cleared or the player cancels them. Canceled and completed scientist tasks are kept in recent task history for review. Task route previews are deliberately quiet: movement and hauling paths are painted on the map only while the Movement overlay is active, with a selected task route taking priority over the next generic queued route.

The keyboard-control policy separates global controls, map controls, and menu command chains. Global controls work from the Map and management menus unless a text input, select, or editable field has focus: Space pauses, `>` increases speed, `<` decreases speed, `.` skips to the next meaningful event, `Ctrl+.` skips to the next scientist queue completion, `?` toggles keyboard help, and Escape backs out in order. Map-only controls are `WASD` for camera panning, `Shift+WASD` for faster panning, arrow keys for the map cursor, `[` and `]` for viewing the layer below or above, Enter for selecting the top-priority target under the cursor, `O` and `Shift+O` for overlay cycling, `+` and `-` for zoom, mouse wheel for zoom, and middle-drag for grab-panning. Top-level letter paths open management surfaces: `M` Map, `F` Foundry, `T` Tasks, `I` Stores, `B` Black Market, `C` Creatures, `P` Policies, `R` Records, and `G` Debug when visible. Submenu letters choose current tabs, such as `T Q` for Tasks > Queue, `I B` for Stores > Byproducts, `B L` for Black Market > Offers, `B K` for Contracts, `B H` for its Ledger, `C C` for Containers, `P F` for Policies > Feeding, and `R M` for Records > Messages. `W`, `A`, `S`, and `D` are reserved for map movement and should not be assigned to menu paths or primary contextual commands. Browser-native keys such as Tab, Ctrl+R, Ctrl+F, Ctrl+L, Alt+Left/Right, and function keys should remain browser/accessibility behavior unless there is a strong future reason to capture them.

The map overlay system keeps one active overlay selected from a temporary map HUD popover or cycled with `O` and `Shift+O`. Current overlays are None, Contamination, Movement, Resources, Incidents, Combat, Construction, and Debug. The popover closes after an overlay or resource focus is chosen, keeping the map surface clear. Overlay legends live inside that temporary menu and are contextual to the active overlay rather than permanently occupying the blueprint. Normal overlays must represent what the scientist or player already knows: contamination uses the current room observation or stale remembered observations, movement uses known queued paths, resources use last-inventoried room supplies for one selected material or category, incidents use known unresolved alerts, combat uses known active or last-known combat records, and construction uses player designations. The Debug overlay is explicitly omniscient for development and can show raw simulation details, including unobserved room conditions and actors. Future overlays should follow the same rule: no free information in normal play, but debug can expose the underlying simulation for testing.

The current browser foundation is still worth keeping for now. HTML and CSS are strong fits for dense management screens, forms, logs, policy tabs, tooltips, inspectors, inventory ledgers, journals, and readable text-heavy UI. A full engine rewrite should not happen unless the project hits a clear technical limit that cannot be solved with better architecture.

The rendering model is hybrid. Canvas owns the physical map, sprites, animation, lighting, effects, zoom, pan, and overlays. HTML remains the layer for menus, panels, inspectors, policy screens, tooltips, and accessibility controls. The DOM tile map remains available only as the compatibility renderer and automatic rollback path.

The approved visual direction uses a fixed north-up orthographic map with shallow elevated drawing cues for walls, fixtures, and actors rather than a true isometric projection. One tile remains one physical square meter regardless of raster size. Future assets should use a hard-edged low-detail illustrated style, approximately 64 source pixels per tile, with semantic strategic, operational, and inspection zoom bands. The ordinary map shows physical materials and lighting; room purposes and other management abstractions belong in overlays. Generated creatures use deterministic modular recipes built from body family, dimensions, Body Consistency, pigmentation, Appendages, restrained Element and Byproduct manifestations, condition, and activity. Simulation masks remain authoritative, tiny creatures may receive minimum-size strategic markers without larger collision, and stale visuals must use last-observed appearance rather than current hidden state. The complete production contract is in `VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`.

Simulation rules should not depend on either the primary Canvas renderer or the DOM compatibility renderer. Game state should be the source of truth. Rendering should read from state rather than becoming state. Player actions should flow through command functions, simulation should advance through explicit systems, and UI code should display derived view models instead of calculating core rules directly.

Future organization should gradually move toward clear boundaries:

- State: defaults, save data, normalization, migration or reset behavior, and schema-like conventions.
- Systems: time, metabolism, movement, autonomous creature activity, contamination diffusion, jobs, incidents, reproduction, decay, and future AI.
- Commands: player intent such as synthesize, move, feed, set a role, open a door, transfer a receptacle, designate excavation, harvest, sell, or change policy.
- Selectors and view models: read-only derived data for panels, tooltips, map overlays, warnings, and contextual actions.
- UI: DOM rendering, event binding, panel layout, hotkeys, command menus, and presentation state.
- Map renderer: a replaceable boundary with Canvas as the primary physical-map renderer and DOM retained as a compatibility fallback, independent of pathfinding, rooms, actors, and simulation logic.

The frontend architecture is now split between authoritative simulation state, renderer-neutral scene construction, and replaceable presentation. Canvas is the primary physical-map renderer, while the DOM Compatibility Map consumes the same semantic model as a persistent fallback. Both remain independent of pathfinding, rooms, actors, commands, and simulation timing. Future passes should continue extracting command functions, selectors, and system modules without coupling game rules to either renderer.

Simulation systems no longer all run every 250 real milliseconds. Tactical work, containment, sensory updates, environment exchange, biology, local group context, actor behavior, and administration use explicit game-time cadences while preserving accumulated elapsed time. Manual time skips flush every system so they do not strand nearly-complete work. Calm contained slimes use slower, deterministically staggered AI and sensory updates; loose, moving, injured, stressed, working, or fighting slimes remain on faster cadences. Local group context runs every five game seconds with deterministic temporary cohorts, strict six-tile neighborhoods, and a 16-neighbor detail cap with aggregate density beyond it. Camera position and visibility never reduce simulation fidelity. Dormancy means less frequent decision-making, not frozen biology or different rules when the player looks away.

Actor contact, nearby magical presence, vision, and common room/container queries use a runtime spatial index rather than repeatedly scanning the complete population. Same-time work is ordered by due time, priority, stable actor ID, and stable event ID so browser speed does not decide outcomes. Accelerated time integrates the full elapsed duration rather than dropping simulation work. Debug Performance displays per-system call and timing samples, rendering and persistence cost, actor and occupied-cell counts, cadence timing, and overdue-system backlog. The group development probe targets 250 living slimes and 100 actively changing slimes as a practical loose benchmark; correctness tests require deterministic results and hard bounded detail, while absolute timing remains advisory across machines. Automated tests cover cadence chunk equivalence, stable event order, and indexed queries across a 1,000-actor fixture.

The map renderer boundary has an explicit semantic cell model. Each map cell describes its base terrain or room, door state, visible object stack, incident marker, route marker, overlay data, selection/cursor/scientist flags, click target, tooltip parts, glyph, `spriteKey`, and `spriteLayers`. Canvas and DOM consume the same semantic cells and route pointer events through the same selection targets. DOM-specific classes, datasets, titles, and text are produced only by the compatibility adapter rather than becoming part of the map model. Tests cover semantic cells and renderer parity so presentation code never has to infer game meaning from DOM styling.

Terrain connectivity is also renderer-neutral derived state. Natural rock, floor surfaces, constructed walls, doors, vertical connectors, future fluids, and room or compartment boundaries remain separate physical or overlay layers. Cardinal edge relations distinguish structural joins, material abutments, portals, surface transitions, exposed edges, unknown neighbors, and map boundaries; diagonal relations refine corners without changing physical connectivity. Natural rock joins continuously across ore deposits, while constructed-to-natural and unlike-material contacts retain visible seams. Door fixtures save their frame orientation when installed so later construction cannot rotate them unexpectedly. Stairs may expose up, down, or both directions on one tile, ramps identify entry, middle, exit, and upper-landing segments, and deterministic coordinate-based variation can break visual repetition without becoming save state. Unknown space caps connectivity instead of leaking hidden terrain.

The map renderer now consumes a versioned, transient `MapScene` rather than treating per-tile DOM output as the complete visual model. A scene covers the visible viewport plus a one-tile overscan margin and contains semantic terrain cells, unique physical entities, knowledge-filtered environment inputs, effects, overlays, selection footprints, and a cell interaction index. Multi-tile actors and fixtures appear once with complete occupied-cell masks; contained occupants remain interaction targets attached to their container rather than becoming duplicate map entities. Entity knowledge is explicitly current, stale, uncertain, unknown, or Debug, and stale records use saved observations rather than current hidden state. CSS classes are produced only by the DOM adapter from semantic door states, object tags, and overlay states. The scene is derived and never saved; simulation and observation records remain authoritative.

Player map knowledge is saved as sparse semantic cell observations. The scientist perceives same-layer cells through one shared exact-perception rule used by the map, creature vision, and incidents: Dark permits contact range, Dim permits four tiles, Lit permits eight, and Bright permits twelve, with physical line of sight still required beyond contact. Walls and closed opaque doors occlude the view, while Debug remains explicitly omniscient. Darkness does not erase explored terrain; a cell outside current perception falls back to its saved memory. Reobserved cells replace their remembered terrain, door, anchor, connectivity, and static-object snapshot. Unobserved changes do not update or visibly flag the old snapshot. Memories age by game time through recent, aged, and archived presentation tiers, but terrain remains remembered. Dynamic subjects use their own observation records: recent loose creatures can project a selectable last-known footprint with no motion or current hidden state, uncertain incidents use approximate cells and radii rather than hidden identities, and exact physical commands require reacquisition. Player-authored plans, routes, and designations remain visible independently of physical perception, and knowledge is independent on every z-layer.

A Canvas 2D renderer now serves as the promoted default and renders the same `MapScene` inside the existing map shell. It draws semantic terrain, glyph placeholders, unique multi-tile entities, knowledge states, effects, overlays, selection, and cursor state without reading simulation records directly. Canvas and DOM both consume the scene's semantic Dark, Dim, Lit, and Bright treatment; lighting shades terrain and subjects without affecting alerts or selection. Dense known airborne mixtures receive a restrained identity-free haze pattern. Temperature, humidity, mana, light, and aggregate contamination remain single selected diagnostic overlays, current readings expose bands rather than exact numbers, and remembered room bands are visibly stale rather than recomputed from hidden state. Exact airborne identities remain Debug-only until physical diagnostic instruments support them. Canvas uses high-DPI backing resolution, bounds-aware one-tile overscan culling, resize observation, and an event-driven animation-frame scheduler rather than a permanent frame loop. Raw entities, effects, and overlays are culled before scene normalization; stable large populations may use a 16-tile chunk query so offscreen records are not allocated into `MapScene`. Canvas compiles visible cells, depth ordering, semantic passes, effects, occlusion sets, and selection cells into a draw plan when the scene changes, then reuses that plan for animation and presentation-only invalidations. Diagnostics expose plan builds/uses, visible and culled counts, and invalidation reasons. Renderer choice is a persistent UI preference independent of save files; Canvas is the promoted default, the DOM Compatibility Map remains player-selectable, and Canvas failures automatically persist a DOM rollback with a visible explanation. Canvas and DOM share tile-aligned camera, zoom, layer, cursor, selection, contextual commands, designation tools, and persistence state. Canvas adds smooth transient sub-tile panning without writing presentation offsets into the save or simulation, keeps its drawing surface alive during navigation, preserves the viewed center through resize, and supports WASD, middle-drag grab panning, pointer-anchored wheel zoom, keyboard zoom, and layer controls. Screen-to-cell and cell-to-screen transforms account for the current visual offset. Pointer hit testing resolves through the knowledge-filtered `MapScene` interaction index, supplemented only by transient rectangular bounds for a known actor that follow its displayed body, rather than querying simulation entities or testing sprite alpha. A shared renderer-neutral policy orders terrain, ground hazards, paths, items, remains, fixtures, actors, overhead structures, effects, fog, alerts, selection, and cursor; same-pass subjects sort by southern footprint edge and stable identity. Tall subjects use a cross-section above their anchor layer, overhead structures cut away around focus, and later-drawn blockers fade when they cover a selected subject without changing physical order. A click selects the topmost known target, repeated clicks on the same cell cycle downward, and the HTML `Also here` list preserves that order and the exact clicked footprint cell. Delayed HTML tooltips display the semantic cell tooltip, ordinary unknown darkness has no interaction target, and one-tile overscan exists only for drawing. Room and access designations support left-drag painting on Canvas, while construction cells retain the existing click-to-toggle workflow. Scene construction and Canvas drawing have separate performance diagnostics so later optimization can target the actual source of delay.

The deterministic map-population benchmark measures world/index creation, spatial query, `MapScene` construction, draw-plan compilation, real Canvas drawing, frequent entity updates, and navigation in quick and full modes. Its representative fixture uses 500 actors, its dense fixture uses 1,000, and its mostly-offscreen fixture uses 5,000 while holding visible counts constant. Representative p95 targets are 12 ms for scene construction and 8 ms for drawing; dense targets are 25 ms and 16.7 ms. Absolute timing is advisory because hardware and browser load vary. Correctness invariants are strict: offscreen scale must preserve visible counts, bounds must include multi-tile and tall records, and an idle renderer must schedule no additional frames. Dirty rectangles remain deferred until measurement shows a need; WebGL remains deferred unless optimized Canvas consistently misses the dense target.

Renderer promotion now has deterministic semantic and visual gates. Six synthetic scenes exercise ordinary terrain and doors, every knowledge/light treatment, effect planes and representative overlays, crowded interaction ordering, tall and multi-tile subjects on another z-layer, and high-contrast sprite presentation at multiple marker and tile sizes. The fixtures feed the actual DOM cell adapter and Canvas draw path, freeze time and motion, and require identical visible semantic cells, entities, effects, primary and crowded targets, selection, cursor, tooltips, render ordering, and accessibility summaries. Renderer-specific screenshots are intentionally separate because the DOM glyph map and Canvas artwork need information equivalence rather than pixel equality. Ubuntu Chromium owns the committed baselines, including a compact-desktop layout; updates are explicit and limited to 0.5-percent pixel variance with a restrained antialiasing threshold. Passing automation, approved Linux screenshots, documented information-equivalent differences, and the completed Canvas-default play soak supported final promotion. Canvas is now the official map renderer; the DOM fallback remains available until a future separate removal decision.

Map effects use three semantic planes shared by DOM and Canvas: ground for physical spills and surface hazards, world for structural failures and active abilities, and alert for known incidents and actionable task endpoints. Every effect is derived from authoritative simulation or player-authored task state and carries stable source identity, affected cells, knowledge, severity, intensity band, optional damage tags and timing, uncertainty radius, semantic glyph/key, and optional interaction targets. Multiple incidents or tasks on one tile collapse into a count-bearing marker while preserving every known target in interaction ordering. Unknown physical hazards do not appear; stale incident records remain subdued, uncertain detections use an area rather than false precision, and player-authored task markers remain visible independently of physical perception.

Persistent equipment condition is not duplicated as a field of ambient map effects. Current fixtures, containers, and doors instead expose at most two severity-ranked semantic status cues, prioritizing breaches, serious blockage, power loss, impaired operation, or damage. Routine switched-off equipment remains in its inspector rather than covering the map in badges. Actors retain their independent pose and condition-cue contract. Procedural fire, electricity, magic, spill, structural, alert, and task treatments are available to both renderers, but the map never invents a fire or electrical hazard merely to exercise artwork. Effects remain static unless an authoritative timed state later justifies restrained event-driven animation.

Canvas movement now uses a presentation-only animation clock anchored to authoritative game time, speed, pause state, and a transient timeline revision. Simulation movement records expose knowledge-filtered segment endpoints and timestamps through `MapScene`; eligible current or Debug actors interpolate linearly only when the segment will remain visible for at least 80 real milliseconds. Faster time, stale knowledge, reduced-motion preference, manual skips, loads, resets, and relocations snap safely. Multi-tile bodies, actor cues, selection outlines, and supplemental known-actor hit bounds move together while occupancy, collision, interaction cells, orientation, and all outcomes remain discrete simulation state. Vertical travel fades rather than floating between layers. Canvas schedules frames only while a visible eligible segment is active, and the DOM map remains tile-snapped.

Map accessibility is a persistent presentation layer shared by both renderers rather than simulation or saved-run state. The HTML Display & Accessibility panel controls sprite-with-fallback or glyph-first map presentation, reduced motion, standard or high contrast, reduced/standard/strong effect intensity, 8/12/16-pixel minimum tile sizes, and 100/125/150-percent marker scale. Canvas glyph-first mode deliberately skips sprite drawing while retaining semantic terrain, entities, status cues, effects, selection, and interaction behavior; the DOM renderer remains a complete glyph map. Operating-system reduced-motion and forced-color settings take precedence. Color is never the sole carrier of map meaning: hazards, effects, statuses, stale knowledge, routes, selection, and cursor state retain glyph, shape, pattern, outline, or line-style cues. Reduced effect intensity has a visibility floor for serious hazards. A single polite assistive status describes the keyboard cursor's coordinates, known cell detail, and selectable target without turning every tile into a tab stop.

Map-only interactions use a scoped render path. Camera movement, zoom, selection, inspector tabs, and contextual command menus redraw the map workspace without rebuilding hidden management screens. Simulation updates now distinguish map-changing work from selection/readout changes: the map grid is rebuilt for movement, combat, incidents, and relevant active overlays, while ordinary stat changes refresh the compact inspector without rebuilding terrain. Open management screens refresh at a bounded cadence instead of once per simulation tick. Per-view terrain indexes such as excavated-cell membership are computed once and shared across visible cells; tile selectors must not rebuild whole-map sets inside per-cell loops.

Background autosaves use real time rather than game time and occur at most once every ten real-world minutes while the run is dirty. Important explicit transactions may still create immediate persistence checkpoints, and the browser attempts a final save during an ordinary close. Simulation ticks never synchronously serialize the complete save every 250 milliseconds.

## Container Compatibility

Containers are physical lab equipment, not generic storage slots. Current prototype containers have base types with interior dimensions, openings, open or sealed geometry, load limits, durability, comfort, drainage, environment exchange, material resistance, and optional wards. Wards modify specific problems rather than solving all containment.

Container readouts separate three related concepts. Physical Fit estimates whether the specimen's size, shape, weight, opening requirements, and body flexibility make sense in the container. Compatibility estimates material resistance and functional support, such as whether the container resists corrosive, toxic, thermal, cold, electrical, or arcane hazards and whether drainage, sealing, open-top geometry, or collection support suits the specimen. Active Containment Risk combines compatibility, current creature condition, stress, hunger, stability, container condition, handling state, corpses, and other pressure sources into the chance of incidents.

Player-facing compatibility remains discovery-aware. Known traits can produce specific notes such as poor corrosive resistance or lack of drainage. Undiscovered traits widen the assessment without naming hidden hazards. The simulation still uses the real hidden biology, so an undiscovered acid slime can slowly stress a poor glass jar even before the scientist understands why.

Poor compatibility has light first-pass consequences rather than catastrophic container destruction. Over time it can raise specimen Stress, foul the container interior, slightly wear unsuitable materials, and add pressure toward minor containment incidents. This keeps container choice meaningful while leaving full repair, replacement, and equipment crafting for later systems.

Contained slimes now have an active containment-testing behavior layer. A slime can begin probing containment because of hunger, high Stress, hostile habitat, recent pain, poor compatibility, dangerous temperament, crowding, weak seals, bad condition, or similar pressure. The UI shows qualitative escape pressure bands, such as Quiet, Low, Moderate, High, and Critical, plus the observed method when active. Methods include pressing, attacking, seeping, corroding, shocking fittings, climbing or gripping, fouling the interior, waiting for a handling mistake, and using unstable elemental force. This behavior is visible without exposing exact hidden formulas or undiscovered trait names.

Containment testing has gradual consequences. Active testing can build warning signs, increase Stress, foul the interior, damage container condition through the existing damage-type and resistance vocabulary, leak trace contamination into the room in severe cases, and give creatures behavior memory or relevant practice such as Striking or elemental damage-skill practice. A full breach is intentionally rare: it requires critical escape pressure, enough repeated progress, and a container that is already nearly ruined.

Full breaches now resolve through physical outcomes instead of a generic escape. Possible results include a slime seeping through a weak seal, climbing out through openings, forcing a latch, cracking a fragile container, dissolving a wall, tipping or spilling an open vessel, or disrupting fittings with unstable elemental force. Containers record `intact`, `compromised`, or `breached` state plus the last breach summary. A compromised container may remain physically usable when that makes sense, while a breached container is excluded from containment until future repair or replacement. The event log and container card report the breach result, and the loose-slime release path places the escapee near the container on the map.

## Tools and Handling Equipment

The scientist begins exactly as the transfer fiction implies: newly spawned from the vat, naked and with no equipped clothing, helmet, backpack, hand lamp, or testing loadout. Starter clothing and ordinary protective equipment are physical items in Storage, not invisible defaults. The empty equipment plan is itself a valid state and can be saved as a named loadout.

Equipment uses shared actor attachment points rather than scientist-only booleans: Head, Eyes, Face, Torso, Hands, Legs, Feet, Main Hand, Off Hand, Waist, two belt attachment points, and Back Mount. Creature bodies can adopt the same model later when their anatomy supports particular points. The Back Mount is deliberately retained. A backpack is one optional use, while tool harnesses, tanks, reservoirs, collection or diagnostic assemblies, warded frames, and articulated tentacle apparatus can compete for that same mount. Equipping one back assembly displaces the other instead of deleting the slot from the design.

Equipping is physical work. The scientist routes to the stored item, carries its durable instance, and occupies its declared attachment point; unequipping is a timed action that retains the item in actor inventory. Player-named loadouts save desired item types rather than teleporting a particular instance, and applying one queues the required removal and retrieval work. Items remain physical, reservable, damageable, maintainable objects throughout.

Worn storage changes mass and volume capacity, while total carried load produces Light, Loaded, or Heavily Burdened bands. High burden slows walking and raises physical stamina costs. Protective clothing mitigates relevant damage types rather than supplying a generic armor number, loses condition when it absorbs harm, and can accumulate contamination. Neutralizing Wash removes that contamination. Equipment summaries, occupied slots, burden, and contamination are visible in the scientist inspector and debug snapshot.

Handling tools are reusable physical objects, not permanent abstract toggles. Thick gloves, long tongs, hook poles, and scrapers now have individual durability, broad material notes, damage resistance, and condition bands: Pristine, Worn, Damaged, Failing, and Broken. Broken tools remain cataloged and cannot be used until repaired. The shared basic maintenance and repair actions can restore compatible handling and construction tools; specialized materials, advanced damage, complete fabrication, and workshop-quality restoration remain future systems.

Tool durability currently wears during meaningful hazardous handling rather than every ordinary use. Opening or closing containers with live specimens, moving living slimes, and dumping or scraping remains can damage the selected handling tool based on the exposed damage tags, the tool's resistance profile, the current condition band, and the handling risk. Wearable handling protection such as Thick Gloves must actually occupy its equipment slot; storing it in a cabinet is not protection. Low condition reduces effective resistance and raises handling risk before the tool fully breaks. Empty safe handling should not grind equipment down just for clicking a button.

The Storage Room inventory ledger shows tool count, usable count, best condition, durability, and material/resistance tooltip details. Handling policy readouts and disabled button reasons distinguish between tools that are not stocked and tools that are stocked but broken. This is a foundation for later repair, replacement, crafting, warded equipment, cleanup tools, combat equipment, and room-object damage.

## Design Vision

Helix Heresy should feel like running an unsafe little arcane biology lab. The player should not start with a complete wiki. They should learn by making creatures, testing them, comparing notes, and steering reproduction or recombination toward goals they only partially understand.

Core design pillars:

- Discovery over disclosure: hidden trait outcomes and gene mappings should be learned through play.
- Strange but readable creatures: generated specimens should be easy to imagine, not just lists of numbers.
- Lab capability matters: skills improve execution, while equipment and resources should eventually unlock what tests are possible.
- Weird biology is allowed: clashing traits can create awkward or weak creatures instead of being forbidden.
- Reproduction and recombination should feel experimental: the player can aim for known traits, but unusual combinations should remain possible.

## Campaign Vision

The full game should become a browser-based roguelike management and simulation game like Dwarf Fortress about illegal life creation in a magical world. The prototype is slime-first, but the larger arc is not slime-only: slimes are the first low-gene creature family before stranger, smarter, more dangerous life forms become possible.

The intended long-term loop:

- Experiment on genes and synthesize creatures.
- Observe results through casual inspection, lab tests, and practical use.
- Manage money, biomass, reagents, lab space, equipment, time, secrecy, and Suspicion.
- Hide evidence, contain accidents, and avoid authority attention.
- Assign creatures to jobs, lab areas, disposal tasks, protection, infiltration, production, or sale.
- Visit the black market to sell creatures, byproducts, services, and eventually fulfill commissions.
- Use discoveries, profits, and creatures to expand from a hidden lab into larger operations.

Starting scenarios should define the initial story and resource position. Examples include a protected corporate black-project lab, a legitimate company job hiding secret desert-base construction, and a sewer fugitive start where the authorities are already hunting the player.

Difficulty should be modular rather than a single preset. Settings can affect journal support, gene visibility, authority aggression, resource scarcity, experiment consequences, mutation chaos, and how learnable the DNA puzzle is. Easy settings can be darkly funny and forgiving; hard settings should become tense, secretive, and resource-starved.

Suspicion is the pressure of being discovered. It should eventually track evidence trails, escaped creatures, lab incidents, black market exposure, and direct government attention. On low difficulty it can be background pressure; on high difficulty it should become a constant survival threat.

The black market should grow with reputation. Early sales may be whatever useful creatures or byproducts the player can produce. As reputation rises, customers should commission creatures with specific traits, behaviors, affinities, or jobs.

The late game should escalate from survival and profit into power. The player can recruit people through money or promises, create stronger creatures and eventually intelligent agents, confront the local government, take control of territory, and transition into a global conquest layer. Higher-intelligence creations should be more useful but also more capable of betrayal and non-gene-driven behavior.

## Creature Vision

Creatures should eventually be described as physical beings with form, dimensions, mass, affinity, behavior, and usefulness.

Slimes are the starting point because they are the most basic experimental creature. The broader game should be able to grow into other creature families with their own body plans, constraints, uses, and risks.

Current direction:

- Shape is a genetic trait that describes the creature's body plan.
- Body consistency is a genetic trait that describes how the creature holds together physically.
- Appendages are a genetic trait separate from shape.
- Size describes shape-aware dimensions instead of only total volume.
- Weight is derived, not genetic.
- Density is derived primarily from elemental affinity and affinity strength.
- Movement is derived from shape, appendages, size, weight, and element instead of being directly genetic.
- Natural byproduct is a genetic/biological output constrained by element and physiology, not by role assignment.
- Natural output intensity affects metabolism through broad hidden bands rather than exact player-facing numbers.

Examples of the intended style:

- `Shape: worm-like`
- `Body Consistency: elastic gel`
- `Appendages: grasping pseudopods`
- `Size: 1.8 m long, 12 cm thick`
- `Weight: 34 kg`
- `Movement: pulls itself forward`
- `Byproduct: corrosive slime`
- `Natural output: Moderate`
- `Metabolic demand: Baseline`

Odd combinations are acceptable. A puddle with stub legs or a metal slime with wing-like membranes may become physically awkward, but that awkwardness can later feed into creature stats, mobility, stability, containment difficulty, or combat/work usefulness.

## Slime AI Direction

Slimes now have a first-pass AI record stored on each living specimen as `slime.ai`. This is a shared behavior-state wrapper, not full intelligence yet. The record stores broad state, intent, target, reason, urgency, path, next decision time, current combat decision, and last update time. Current broad states include contained, idle, moving, seeking, feeding, working, blocked, combat, stressed, and dead.

Slimes have drive records stored at `slime.ai.drives`. Current drive categories are Hunger, Regrowth, Injury, Stress, Containment, and Division Readiness. Work is not a biological drive. A creature's Role is a player intention tag that categorizes intended use and observed performance; it does not compel an unintelligent slime to act or bias its biological decisions. A satiated or seriously threatened slime can therefore ignore physically available food even while it has a consumptive Role. Division Readiness is a physiological process rather than a chosen nesting intention: a slime that satisfies the established mass, nutrition, integrity, stress, maturity, and sustained-fullness requirements divides wherever it already is.

Slimes do not sleep. Their low-pressure baseline is quiescence, reflecting their plantlike and sessile-organism biology. Quiescence permits the normal recovery, regrowth, environmental feeding, and calming systems to operate without inventing a sleep need. Injury can produce an active recovery intent, but a slime does not seek a bed or accumulate fatigue merely because it has remained active.

The shared decision model separates drives, perceived opportunities, committed intentions, and immediate physical actions. Drives create motivation without granting information. Perception and actionable memory provide possible targets. The slime eliminates unavailable or temporarily avoided opportunities, compares the remaining responses, commits to an intent, and uses movement or local interaction to pursue it. A short commitment window and a preference for the current target prevent indecisive oscillation, while immediate danger, damage, target loss, or a physically blocked route can force reevaluation. Healthy hungry slimes that lack a resolved food gradient may explore in search of food, but the intent remains food-seeking rather than generic wandering.

Threat interrupts feeding and ordinary movement. A frightened slime chooses fight, flight, or freeze according to aggression, desperation, stability, injury, pain memory, relative threat, kinship, and available escape rather than continuing to eat through danger. Chronic injury without a current threat produces recovery instead of an invented opponent. Harmless failures create a short target-specific retry delay, currently about 15 in-game minutes. Painful failures create several hours of avoidance, and repeated failures strengthen the delay. Failure memory records the target, reason, location, severity, time, and retry window so a blocked route does not erase belief in the underlying food or stimulus.

Scientists and creatures now use a shared sensory model. Each actor has explicit capabilities, hidden sensitivity values, current perceptions, one-hour actionable memories, a bounded 100-entry sensory log, and persistent bounded route memory. Repeated sensory ticks are coalesced instead of flooding the log. Normal player rendering only shows what the scientist knows; exact biological sensitivity and slime sensory logs are Debug information.

Slimes are blind and deaf. They universally possess chemical sensing, taste, physical contact, and magical sensitivity, with individual hidden biological sensitivity strengths. They detect physical volatile traces that diffuse tile by tile with airflow, leak according to doors and container seals, and decay over time. Taste and contact are exact only when the slime shares physical space with a source. Magical sensitivity detects mana gradients, magical presences, active effects, and supernatural infrastructure without revealing ordinary geometry or granting vision. `Perception` is general trained awareness, while `Arcane Senses` is trained interpretation of magical stimuli.

The scientist has vision, hearing, smell/chemical awareness, taste, contact, and magical sensitivity. Vision is exact but requires range, line of sight, and sufficient light. Hearing is directional and approximate. Chemical cues are broad gradients. Magical cues provide approximate direction and intensity. All channels use the same one-hour actionable-memory duration, but retain their distinct precision. Only detected serious/critical incidents and combat alerts appear on the map; routine stimuli remain in actor logs. Approximate detections create uncertain incident locations that can later be refined by exact sight or contact.

Loose slime food-seeking no longer uses exact room-wide resource knowledge. Slimes consume only contacted material, follow locally stronger physical chemical gradients one step at a time, and may retrace previously traversed cells toward a remembered cue. Habitat seeking likewise compares local adjacent conditions. Closed doors remain physical barriers even when traces leak through them.

The implementation deliberately preserves existing `roomActivity`, `autonomousMovement`, role, container, and containment-risk execution while placing the shared decision layer around them. `slime.ai.commitment` stores the active intent, target, reason, urgency, start, and minimum commitment time. `slime.ai.failures` stores bounded target-specific retry memories. `slime.autonomousMovement` remains the route execution record for loose slimes; it stores intent, target, path, target cell, route distance, movement speed, condition modifiers, start time, and arrival time. Movement speed is derived from physical movement biology and then modified by current maturity, mass, Body Integrity, and Stress. Autonomous safety reevaluation occurs at least every 30 in-game seconds, while damage, combat contact, blocked movement, and other important events can trigger it immediately.

Player-facing AI readouts remain broad and readable, such as `AI: Moving - seeking loose biomatter` or `AI: Blocked - blocked from Loose biomatter`. The player receives no live omniscient intent feed: current state is refreshed only while the scientist observes the slime, and an unobserved slime retains its last observed state and timestamp. Observed intent changes enter full message history. Routine changes stay out of the compact feed, while notable combat, flight, freezing, panic, and blockage may appear there. Unobserved transitions remain internal and Debug-only. The selected slime card explains broad state, intent, target, urgency, observed pressures, path status, next decision timing, and unknown-factor warnings without exposing exact hidden weights. Its Group Behavior panel shows local tendency, aggregate density, temporary cohort size, personal territory, and physical distress response without exposing a shared target or hidden formula. The Debug workspace exposes raw state, commitment, failure memories, actual perception, response scores, combat decisions, bounded social context, temporary cohort IDs, group targets, territorial memory, room activity, movement, and containment testing.

Future slime AI passes should build on this foundation without turning roles into commands: needs and drives choose intent, perception discovers possible targets, commitment controls switching, autonomous movement executes map goals, feeding consumes contacted resources, habitat response adjusts comfort and movement, and containment, combat, physical work, and social behavior use the same state model.

## Adaptive Skills and Abilities

Skills are practiced domains. They represent something a creature, scientist, or other actor can actively practice, apply, refine, or perform. They are not passive biology and they are not every derived gameplay result.

Stats describe hidden biological capacity. Traits describe biological identity. Skills describe practiced capability. Abilities describe specific actions, techniques, procedures, or maneuvers made possible by skills. Outcomes describe what happens when traits, hidden stats, skills, current condition, equipment, and environment interact.

A skill should be something the actor can practice. An ability should be something the actor can do. An outcome should be something the game calculates.

Level 0 skills should not appear on character sheets. If an actor has not reached level 1 in a skill, that skill does not exist for that actor in player-facing UI. Practice below level 1 can exist as hidden progress, but the sheet should only populate once a skill becomes an Initiate skill.

Skills now use a shared curve with an effective cap of level 320. Tiers are Initiate 1-50, Novice 51-100, Adept 101-150, Master 151-200, Heroic 201-250, Legendary 251-300, and Divine 301-320. Breakthroughs happen automatically at 0 -> 1 and every 50-level tier boundary after that. A breakthrough costs the normal level cost from 20 levels higher; for example, 0 -> 1 uses the normal 20 -> 21 cost, and 50 -> 51 uses the normal 70 -> 71 cost. Overflow XP does not carry through a breakthrough, so crossing into a new tier starts clean at 0 XP toward the next level. Breakthrough progress decays only while the actor is sitting at a breakthrough threshold: after 24 in-game hours without practicing that skill, stored threshold progress decays by 10% of the current breakthrough requirement per in-game day. Earned levels do not decay. Failed or low-confidence actions can still teach, but they award reduced XP; the current prototype uses full XP for success, half XP for partial results, one-quarter XP for failure, and no XP for cancelled actions.

Base skills should be broad world-scale domains, not narrow lab job titles. The current prototype scientist skill domains are:

- Fabrication

These names should make sense beyond the starting scientist. Hunters, soldiers, nobles, monsters, assistants, and future non-slime creatures should be able to use the same system. More specialized names belong at higher tiers or after lived specialization. For example:

- Fabrication can later evolve into Biofabrication, Container Fabrication, Warded Fabrication, or Ritual Fabrication.

- market value

Byproduct production is not a skill. A slime does not train byproduct control simply by existing and producing its natural output. Natural byproduct production is biology and condition-driven output. Skills may influence related procedures or active abilities, but routine production itself is not a skill.

Movement is not a skill as a broad category. Specific practiced forms such as climbing, swimming, burrowing, squeezing, dodging, or pouncing can be skills if they matter mechanically and can improve through use.

The current first-pass creature skill foundation stores slime skill practice hidden from the normal specimen sheet until Analyze exists. Slimes can currently gain hidden practice from meaningful actions such as perceiving stimuli, surviving combat damage, striking, elemental clashes, elemental attacks, and fleeing from remembered danger. Level 0 practice remains invisible and does not count as an existing displayed skill. Normal sustenance, digestion, passive byproduct production, and generic movement are not skills.

Basic tools such as gloves, tongs, hook poles, scrapers, trays, clamps, and simple restraints can belong under broad world skills such as Creature Handling, Fabrication, or future tool-family skills. More complex, dangerous, or specialized tools and weapons can have their own skill families, such as Blades, Axes, Firearms, Surgery, Animancy, or specialized machinery.

Elemental skill names should remain flexible. The tier should be metadata, not forced grammar. Skills should be displayed as a skill name with a tier tag, such as Flame [Initiate], Smoldering Flame [Novice], Electricity [Initiate], Arcing Current [Adept], Acid [Initiate], or Corrosive Acid [Novice]. This avoids trapping all skills into a rigid Initiate-of-X naming pattern.

Specific elemental techniques such as Flame Jet, Shock Arc, Acid Spit, or Heat Pulse are abilities derived from elemental skills, not the skills themselves. A creature might have Flame [Initiate] and later evolve it into Smoldering Flame [Novice], gaining or improving abilities such as Smoke Vent, Ember Cloud, or Choking Heat. Another creature might evolve the same initial skill into a different Novice form and fight very differently.

Skills should evolve based on use, pressure, and lived experience. Two creatures with similar species, genes, or starting skills can become different because they practiced different things, survived different situations, or were shaped by different environments. The goal is that the same monster type should not always imply the same fight.

Skill tiers are Initiate, Novice, Adept, Master, Heroic, Legendary, and Divine. Each tier represents both advancement and increasing specialization. Higher-tier skill names should reflect the path that shaped them, not only numerical improvement.

Analysis is the parent skill for interpreting practiced capability. Analyze is the base magical ability derived from Analysis. All magical abilities should consume Mana and be basically instant unless a specific ability has a charge-up, ritual, or wind-up reason.

The scientist starts with Analysis [Initiate], level 1, which grants base Analyze. Base Analyze can be used on a living specimen in the lab UI. It costs Mana immediately and reveals only practiced creature capabilities that already exist at level 1 or above, plus broad learned behavior memories. Base Analyze shows skill name and tier, such as Toughness [Initiate], but not exact levels. It does not reveal level 0 practice, raw XP, stats, genes, hidden biological traits, exact formulas, hidden output values, weaknesses, or compatibility formulas.

Analyze output is ability-defined, not continuously determined by the current level of Analysis. At the Novice breakthrough, level 51, Analysis evolves based on how its XP was earned during the previous tier. Creature-focused practice evolves it into Creature Analysis, which grants Advanced Analyze. Combat-shaped practice evolves it into Combat Analysis, which grants Combat Analyze. Necropsy, diagnostic, corpse, tissue, and evidence work evolves it into Forensic Analysis, which grants Forensic Analyze. Material and tool-heavy practice can evolve it into Material Analysis as a future path. The evolved name is displayed as the skill name, while the broad parent remains Analysis internally.

Advanced Analyze costs Mana and reveals exact levels only for skills that base Analyze has already identified; it does not discover new hidden skills by itself. Combat Analyze costs Mana and reads a living target's broad combat intent, threat band, target/action, and observed or obvious damage tags without revealing genes, hidden stats, formulas, or exact weaknesses. Forensic Analyze costs Mana and reads corpse evidence: broad cause, tissue state, harvest/evidence usefulness, and storage risk. Later Analysis evolutions can add Deep Analyze, material reads, ability hints, or evolution-path reads. Base Analyze should remain a stable, simple read.

Creature skill evolution now happens immediately at tier breakthrough. The game chooses an evolved display label from the skill's practice tags, such as Lashing Strikes for combat-shaped Striking, Prying Strikes for containment-shaped Striking, Scarred Toughness for injury-shaped Toughness, or Dissolving Touch for containment-shaped Corrosive practice. The parent skill ID remains broad internally, so systems can still reason about Striking, Toughness, Corrosive, and similar domains without treating passive biology as fake skills.

The player may eventually see information like:

- Toughness [Initiate]
- Toughness [Initiate], level 3 after Advanced Analyze
- Pouncing [Novice]
- Corrosive [Initiate]
- Combat Analysis [Novice], level 61

The player should not see information like:

- Strength: 18
- acid potency: 1.34
- hidden stress value
- genetic byproduct slot
- weak to mineral solvents
- container compatibility multiplier

Genes and traits define biological possibility. Hidden stats define capacity. Skills define practiced capability. Abilities define specific actions. Outcomes are produced when all of those things interact with the world.

## Development Notes

- Keep the game runtime dependency-free unless a feature clearly needs a library.
- Preserve the discovery loop by avoiding public documentation of exact trait outcomes or hidden gene mappings.
- Favor small commits after meaningful feature passes or bug fixes.
- This is a prototype, so systems may be renamed or reshaped as the design becomes clearer.
- Prefer gradual architecture refactors over a full engine rewrite unless the browser foundation reaches a clear technical limit.
- Do not update the changelog for every prototype tweak; reserve it for milestone-ready versions.
